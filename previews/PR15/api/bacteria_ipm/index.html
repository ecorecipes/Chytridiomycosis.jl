Two-trait IPM: Bd and bacteria

New model

This model is new work in this package, not a published result. The Bd side and the zoospore pool are published models (Wilber et al. 2016; Wilber et al. 2017) with values from the compendium. The bacterial decline, its variability and the bacterial load just after a bath come from this package's fit to the Knapp et al. (2022) swabs (below). The protective effects are illustrative, and that fit found no support for an effect on Bd growth.

Each frog carries two continuous traits: its Bd load x (ln zoospore equivalents per swab, as in Wilber et al. 2016) and the load y of an applied antifungal bacterium, Janthinobacterium lividum (ln genomic equivalents per swab). The treated/untreated models (Microbiome treatment) give every treated frog the same protection, and the abundance model gives each frog a bacterial abundance but a single Bd load equation. Here the whole joint distribution of the two loads is projected, so frogs with many bacteria and frogs with few, and their Bd loads, are followed together.

The model

Frogs are uninfected or infected, and carry the bacteria or not. A frog loses its bacteria when y falls below the detection floor (1 genomic equivalent per swab, the lower edge of the y mesh). Every 3 days, with h(y) the protection given by the bacteria,

\[h(y) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(y - y_{50})/w}},\]

an infected frog survives with logit s = b00 + b10 x + κs h(y) (tolerance), clears Bd with logit l = b02 + b12 x + b22 T + κl h(y), and otherwise moves to

\[x' \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(b_{01} + b_{11} x + b_{21} T - κ_g h(y),\; v_{01} e^{2 c_{01} x}\right), \qquad y' \mid x' \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(y - δΔt - ρ x + r\frac{σ_y}{σ_x}(x' - \mathrm{E}x'),\; σ_y^2 (1 - r^2)\right).\]

The bacteria act on Bd through h(y) (survival, growth, clearance and, optionally, the infection probability), and Bd acts on the bacteria through ρ and the correlation r. Frogs without bacteria acquire them from the pond and from other frogs, and a probiotic bath gives a fraction of all frogs a fresh load. Bd spreads through the zoospore pool of Wilber et al. (2017).

The protective threshold has empirical support: Becker et al. (2009) report that "a threshold violacein concentration of about 18 μM on a salamander's skin prevents mortality", and cite a violacein MIC against Bd of 1.82 μM (Brucker et al. 2008). They did not measure Bd load, and J. lividum load predicted violacein only weakly (r = 0.33), so where the threshold lies on the load axis is not known: y50 is illustrative.

With every κ, ρ and r zero the two loads are independent and the Bd side is exactly the Wilber et al. (2016) IPM (tested). Because the Bd axis uses the midpoint rule, as Wilber et al. did, some frogs are lost where the load distribution is narrow; the bacterial axis uses exact bin integrals.

Tolerance and resistance pull R0 in opposite directions: bacteria that let infected frogs survive longer at a given load (κ_survival) raise R0, because those frogs shed for longer, while bacteria that slow Bd growth (κ_growth) lower it.

Fit to the Knapp et al. (2022) cohort

The frogs of the Dusy Basin trial were group-marked, so a frog's two loads cannot be followed from visit to visit. KnappJointModel fits the model to the joint distribution of Bd and J. lividum loads in the treated group on days 12, 37 and 56, starting from its day-0 swabs and a bath of every frog (a new fit, not a published analysis; scripts/bacteria_ipm_knapp_fit.jl). Infection is a constant probability per step, the temperature is fixed at 15 °C, and ITS copies are converted to zoospore equivalents with a fitted copy number within the range of Longo et al. (2013).

Without coupling (coupled = false) the fit gives a J. lividum half-life of 4.07 days, a post-bath load of ln GE 6.5 (SD 2.1), σy = 0.29 per step, an infection probability of 0.50 per step and 64 ITS copies per zoospore equivalent; these are the defaults of BacteriaIPMParameters. The half-life and σy trade off along a nearly flat ridge (4.68 days and σy = 0.15 fit almost as well, log-likelihood −540.40 against −540.38): with group-marked frogs the cross-sections do not separate a slower, quieter decline from a faster, noisier one. Letting the bacteria act on Bd growth finds no protective effect: with κ_growth fixed anywhere between −2 and 4 (and the threshold re-fitted) the log-likelihood is within 0.6 of the uncoupled fit. The unconstrained maximum (13 log-likelihood units better) puts an implausibly large effect in the opposite direction (κ_growth ≈ −107 above ln GE 10.4), carried by a few swabs. Jani & Briggs (2014) likewise found Janthinobacterium positively associated with Bd in an infection experiment, and not correlated with it in the field.

Chytridiomycosis.IPMMesh2D — Type
IPMMesh2D(x::IPMMesh, y::IPMMesh)

The product of two one-trait meshes, for an IPM in which each host carries two continuous traits x and y. It has x.n × y.n bins. A density on it is stored as a vector of length x.n * y.n with x fastest: bin (i, k) has linear index i + (k − 1) x.n, so reshape(v, x.n, y.n) gives the matrix with rows for x and columns for y. meshpoints(m) returns the two vectors of bin midpoints.

Chytridiomycosis.BacteriaIPMProblem — Type
BacteriaIPMProblem(p::BacteriaIPMParameters; temperature,
                   xmesh = IPMMesh(:wilber2016; n = 50), ymesh = IPMMesh(0.0, 14.0, 28),
                   uninfected = 100, infected = 0, zoospores = 0, days = 120,
                   baths = (), transmission = :pool, φ = nothing, u0 = nothing)

A season of the two-trait Bd–bacteria IPM (a new model; see BacteriaIPMParameters) for adult frogs without births, in steps of 3 days.

  • temperature: °C, a number or a function of time in days.
  • xmesh: ln Bd load (ZE per swab); ymesh: ln J. lividum load (GE per swab), whose lower edge is the detection floor below which a frog has lost its bacteria.
  • uninfected, infected, zoospores: the starting frogs, all without bacteria (infected frogs at the initial-load distribution of Wilber et al. 2016), and zoospores in the pond. u0 gives a full starting state instead (layout below).
  • baths: probiotic baths, each (day = d, coverage = f): at the start of the step beginning on day d (rounded down to a step), a fraction f of every class gets a fresh bacterial load y ~ Normal(bath_mean, bath_sd).
  • transmission: :pool (zoospore pool of Wilber et al. 2017), :wilber2016 (the regression φ(T) of frogs housed singly) or :constant (infection probability φ per step, from a pond whose zoospores are not modelled). For the last two the pool is still tracked but does not infect.

The state is u = [S0; S(y); I0(x); I(x, y); Z]: uninfected without bacteria, uninfected per y bin, infected without bacteria per x bin, infected per product bin (x fastest; see IPMMesh2D), and zoospores. solve(prob) returns a BacteriaIPMSolution.

Chytridiomycosis.basic_reproduction_number — Method
basic_reproduction_number(prob::BacteriaIPMProblem; temperature = nothing,
                          as_coded = true) -> Float64

The basic reproduction number of Bd in the uninfected frogs of prob.u0 (with their bacteria as given there), for transmission = :pool: the expected number of frogs infected by one newly infected frog, through the zoospore pool and (for :zpool_dd and :zpool_fd) directly, over its infection. It is the dominant eigenvalue of the next-generation matrix F (I − T)⁻¹ of the step linearised at no Bd (ln(Z + 1) ≈ Z), where T holds the changes of infected frogs (both loads; colonisation at the rate of u0) and of the pool, and F the new infections. Every new infection enters through the same infection probability, so F has rank one and R0 = cᵀ (I − T)⁻¹ n, with n the distribution of newly infected frogs over both loads. The bacteria of uninfected frogs are held as in u0 (no decline), so this is R0 for Bd arriving at that moment. temperature defaults to the temperature at time 0.

as_coded = true (default) follows the R0 code of Wilber et al. (2017), as basic_reproduction_number(::Wilber2017Parameters) does: shedding ln(eˣ + 1) zoospores per infected frog and transmission per step without the factor Δt. Linearising ln(Z + 1) at Z = 0 with shedding μA eˣ (as_coded = false, the model as simulated) counts every zoospore of a frog shedding thousands as if the pool were nearly empty, and gives values in the millions. With no bacteria, as_coded = true equals that function's calculation on the Wilber et al. (2016) kernel (tested).

Chytridiomycosis.KnappJointModel — Type
KnappJointModel(; coupled = true, temperature = 15.0, swabs = load_knapp2022(),
                xmesh = IPMMesh(-5.0, 18.0, 30), ymesh = IPMMesh(0.0, 14.0, 20),
                base = BacteriaIPMParameters(κ_survival = 0.0, κ_growth = 0.0))

The two-trait Bd–bacteria IPM (BacteriaIPMProblem) as a likelihood for the joint Bd and J. lividum loads of the treated cohort of Knapp et al. (2022): a new fit, not a published analysis. The cohort starts from its day-0 swabs, is bathed on day 0, is infected with a constant probability φ per 3-day step, and is compared with the swabs of days 12, 37 and 56 (classes and joint densities among live frogs).

Parameters (natural scale; parameter_names): decline (per day), bath_mean, bath_sd, σy, φ, copies_per_ze (ITS copies per zoospore equivalent, between the lowest and highest of Longo et al. 2013) and, if coupled, κ_growth and y50. Other values are those of base. The log density takes the unconstrained vector x (log, logit and log-ratio transforms); maximum_likelihood fits it.
