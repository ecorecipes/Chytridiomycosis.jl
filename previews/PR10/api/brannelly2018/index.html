Multistate CMR and POPAN (Brannelly 2018)

Brannelly, Chatfield, Sonn, Robak and Richards-Zawacki (2018, BMC Ecology 18:34, doi:10.1186/s12898-018-0189-5) marked northern cricket frogs (Acris crepitans) for a year in a lowland subtropical forest in Louisiana and swabbed them at every capture. They fitted conditional Arnason–Schwarz multistate models (M-SURGE) with two states, Bd-negative and Bd-positive, and a POPAN model (Program MARK) for the super-population size. Infection made frogs easier to catch but did not lower monthly survival (0.931 uninfected, 0.922 infected, model-averaged).

This is a reimplementation of a published model. The capture histories are the paper's Additional file 3 (CC0), bundled in data/brannelly2018. The refit:

  • matches the capture counts of Table 1, except the number of individuals (315 animals in the file, 266 printed);
  • matches every parameter count of Table 2 and the ranking of its three best models;
  • comes within 0.4% of the printed deviances (488.87 against 487.15 for the best model);
  • gives model-averaged survival of 0.928 and 0.923, against the printed 0.931 and 0.922;
  • does not reproduce the super-population size (1394 against 1854). The first capture probability goes to its bound, so N is weakly identified.

Open-population capture–recapture layer

These functions extend the multistate layer.

ArnasonSchwarzProblem is the conditional Arnason–Schwarz model. It conditions on the state at first capture and estimates no initial-state parameters. Survival is per unit time, raised to the length of each interval. Its designs are the MultiEventStructure ones.

PopanProblem is the Schwarz–Arnason (1996) Jolly–Seber formulation. It has entry probabilities pent and a super-population size N = u + exp(θ_N).

Chytridiomycosis.ArnasonSchwarzProblem — Type
ArnasonSchwarzProblem(histories, structure::MultiEventStructure; intervals = ones(T − 1))

The conditional Arnason–Schwarz multistate model (as fitted by M-SURGE and MARK): histories is an n×T matrix with 0 (not caught) or the state k ∈ 1…K of an animal at capture, and structure gives the survival, transition and detection designs (its assignment must be the K×K identity: states are observed without error). The likelihood is conditioned on the first capture and on the state there, so, unlike MultiEventProblem, no initial-state parameter is estimated. Survival is per unit time: over interval t of length intervals[t] it is S^intervals[t]; transitions and detection are per interval and per occasion.

A LogDensityProblems target (log-likelihood, flat prior on the unconstrained parameters prob.names); solve(prob; x0, iters) maximises it and returns an ArnasonSchwarzFit.

Chytridiomycosis.PopanProblem — Type
PopanProblem(histories; survival = :constant, detection = :time, entry = :time,
             intervals = ones(T − 1))

The POPAN (Schwarz & Arnason 1996) open-population model with super-population size N. histories is an n×T matrix; any positive entry is a capture (states are ignored). Each of survival (φ, per unit time, φ^τ over an interval of length τ), detection (p at each of the T occasions) and entry (pent_2…pent_T, multinomial logit with pent_1 as baseline) is :constant (MARK's "(.)") or :time ("(t)"). N = u + exp(θ_N), u the number of distinct animals caught.

The log density is the full multinomial log-likelihood (with its N!/(N − u)! and 1/Π n_ω! terms) at the unconstrained parameters prob.names. solve(prob) returns a PopanFit; popan_parameters gives the natural-scale parameters.

Akaike weights exp(−Δᵢ/2) / Σⱼ exp(−Δⱼ/2) of a set of models with information criteria aic (Δᵢ = aicᵢ − min aic; Burnham & Anderson 2002). A model-averaged estimate is sum(weights .* estimates).

Brannelly et al. 2018

Occasions and intervals

The paper combined each month's two surveys into one occasion. This gives 10 occasions, which is what Table 2's parameter counts require. January, May and July were not surveyed, so the intervals are 1, 2, 1, 1, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1 months.

Two choices were not stated in the paper:

  • Late September and early October. 28 Sep and 1 Oct 2011 can be taken as September's survey pair (the default) or split by calendar month.
  • Interval lengths. Survival can be raised to each interval's length in months (the default) or every interval can count as one step.

The defaults come closest to the printed deviances (see load_brannelly2018).

Table 2 models

In this layer's terms, "f * to" (one parameter for each kind of transition) is :state, and "+ t" adds an additive time effect, :state_plus_time.

Brannelly2018Data

Capture–recapture data of Brannelly et al. (2018) from load_brannelly2018:

  • ids: animal IDs (one per row);
  • survey_dates: the 19 survey days (Dates);

Chytridiomycosis.load_brannelly2018 — Function
load_brannelly2018(; path = brannelly2018_path(), occasions = :survey_pairs,
                   intervals = :months) -> Brannelly2018Data

Read Additional file 3 (capture histories over 19 survey days) and collapse it to 10 monthly occasions, as the paper did. occasions = :survey_pairs (default) takes the two survey days of each month, with 28 Sep and 1 Oct 2011 as September; :calendar_month groups by calendar month. intervals = :months (default) gives the interval lengths in months (1, 2, 1, 1, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1); :equal gives ones. See the source header for these choices.

Chytridiomycosis.brannelly2018_survival — Function
brannelly2018_survival(; data = load_brannelly2018(), models = 1:3) -> NamedTuple

Model-averaged monthly survival of Bd-negative (uninfected) and Bd-positive (infected) frogs over models (default: the three models within 2 AIC of the best in Table 2, which the paper averaged), with Akaike weights renormalised over those models ("natural averaging", Burnham & Anderson 2002).
