Swab loads: units, likelihoods and observation operators

A field swab passes through a chain before it becomes a number: the swab picks up part of the infection, the DNA is extracted and part of the extract is run in a qPCR, the cycle threshold is read off a standard curve, and the result is scaled to the whole swab. This page covers the end of that chain, which the package can state explicitly, and the link from model states to what swabs report. It builds on the load types of Units and loads.

Conversions the sources do not give, and the package refuses: ZE per swab to sporangia per host (the Briggs et al. 2010 state), ZE per swab to zoospores on the whole host (needs a swab recovery fraction nobody has measured for these systems), and qPCR DNA to live zoospores outside the one lab study of Maguire et al. (2016). Each needs an explicit keyword; the sporangia conversion also warns.

The units map

ZE (zoospore equivalents) are a qPCR quantity scaled against standards made from counted zoospores of one strain. The eight strains of Longo et al. (2013) carry 10 to 144 ITS1 copies per zoospore, so the same swab reads up to 14.4-fold differently depending on which strain the lab used for its standards:

strain_copy_number("JEL404")                          # 39.0 copies per zoospore
restandardize(ZEPerSwab(1000), "JEL404" => "CLFT023") # ZEPerSwab(270.8)
restandardize(ZEPerSwab(1000), "JEL404" => "MexMkt")  # ZEPerSwab(3900.0)

c = QPCRStandardCurve(:longo2013, "MexMkt")           # Ct = -3.31 log10(q) + 33.9
qpcr_efficiency(c)                                    # 1.005 (Table 1: 101%)
standard_quantity(c, 30.0)                            # 15.1 zoospores in the reaction

Chestnut et al. (2014) divided their water-sample qPCR quantities by 169 copies per zoospore, above every strain of Longo et al.; restandardize(x, 169 => "CLFT023") re-expresses their densities on a CLFT023 basis (1.17-fold higher).

What restandardize cannot fix: a ZE value assumes the infecting strain has the standard's copy number. When it does not, ZE count ITS1 copies, not zoospores, and only a strain-specific copy number for the field strain would turn them into zoospores.

Chytridiomycosis.QPCRStandardCurve — Type
QPCRStandardCurve(slope, intercept, basis)
QPCRStandardCurve(:longo2013, strain; standards = :zoospores)

A qPCR standard curve Ct = slope · log10(q) + intercept, where q is the quantity of template in the reaction on the stated basis: :zoospores (zoospore genomic equivalents, from counted zoospores) or :dna_ng_per_uL (equimolar genomic DNA).

The keyed form reads the curves of Longo et al. (2013, Table 1) for one of the eight strains of longo_strains: standards = :zoospores is Standard Set A and standards = :dna is Standard Set B. See qpcr_efficiency, standard_quantity and cycle_threshold.

Chytridiomycosis.strain_copy_number — Function
strain_copy_number(strain) -> Float64

ITS1 (ITS1/5.8S) copies per zoospore of a Longo et al. (2013) strain, measured against an amplicon standard (Fig. 4A): from 10 (MexMkt) to 144 (CLFT023). This is the copies_per_zoospore of convert_load for loads quantified with that strain's zoospore standards.

source
Chytridiomycosis.restandardize — Function
restandardize(x::ZEPerSwab, from => to) -> ZEPerSwab

Re-express a load in zoospore equivalents (ZE) measured against the zoospore standards of one strain as ZE of another. from and to are Longo et al. (2013) strain names, or ITS1 copy numbers per zoospore. The swab holds x × n_from ITS1 copies, which is x × n_from / n_to ZE of the other strain: two explicit convert_load steps through ITS1CopiesPerSwab.

For example, a swab read as 1,000 ZE against JEL404 standards (39 copies per zoospore) is 271 ZE against CLFT023 standards (144 copies), and 3,900 ZE against MexMkt standards (10). It assumes, like the standards themselves, that the swab's strain has the standard's copy number; when the infecting strain differs, ZE count copies, not zoospores.

source
Chytridiomycosis.dsdna_mass — Function
dsdna_mass(bp; bp_mass = nothing) -> Float64

Mass in ng of one molecule of double-stranded DNA of bp base pairs. By default the standard formula (617.96 bp + 36.04) g/mol; with bp_mass (Da per base pair) it is bp × bp_mass g/mol, the form Longo et al. (2013) print (bp_mass = 660). Divided by Avogadro's number and scaled to ng.

The copy number of an amplicon standard is its DNA mass divided by this, so the choice of formula scales every copy number derived from it.

source
Chytridiomycosis.genome_equivalents — Function
genome_equivalents(ng; genome_bp, bp_mass = nothing) -> Float64

The number of haploid-set genomes (zoospores, for Bd) whose DNA weighs ng nanograms, for a genome of genome_bp base pairs per zoospore (dsdna_mass gives the mass of one). Longo et al. (2013) use a 46 Mb diploid genome and 660 Da per base pair: genome_equivalents(0.3; genome_bp = 46e6, bp_mass = 660) is about 5,950, their "~6,000 zoospores" per µL of the top DNA standard.

source
Chytridiomycosis.shed_viability — Function
shed_viability(day = 0; key = :maguire2016, reference = true) -> Float64

The fraction of zoospores shed from a live frog that are alive (stain excludes propidium iodide), from the mixed logistic regression of Maguire et al. (2016, Table 2): logit v = b0 + b_day · day for the reference species, plus the species effect and the day × species interaction with reference = false. Coefficients from the compendium.

qPCR counts the DNA of dead zoospores too. A model whose pool holds live zoospores predicts a qPCR (DNA) count of live / v; qpcr_zoospores and viable_fraction do this for the zoospore-pool models.

The table does not name the reference species. The text says the odds of viability rose for Atelopus zeteki and not for Hylomantis lemur, which fits A. zeteki as the reference. On day 0 it is 0.78, the "approximately 80%" of the abstract. One lab study of one strain (JEL 423): not a general constant.

Load likelihoods

LoadHurdleModel is the shared swab likelihood: a fraction π of swabbed hosts is infected, an infected host's load follows a lognormal, gamma, Weibull or negative binomial distribution, and loads below a limit of detection are left-censored:

\[P(\text{non-detect}) = (1-\pi) + \pi\,P(Y < L), \qquad f(y \ge L) = \pi\, f_D(y).\]

With lod = 0 it is the classic hurdle (two-part) model. On scale = :count every family gives the probability of the rounded-up load, so the negative binomial can be compared with the continuous families by AIC; on the continuous scale only the continuous families are comparable. The model implements LogDensityProblems (flat prior on the unconstrained parameters), so it can be sampled with any LogDensityProblems sampler or fitted with maximum_likelihood(m), which also has a method for it (see Fitting to field data); pointwise_loglikelihood(m, x) feeds psis_loo.

y = [s.bd for s in load_knapp2022() if s.group === :wild && !isnan(s.bd)]  # ITS1 copies per swab
f = maximum_likelihood(LoadHurdleModel(y; family = :lognormal))
f.prevalence, f.distribution, f.aic
compare_load_families(y)        # positive loads only, as Schrock et al. fitted each group
Chytridiomycosis.LoadHurdleModel — Type
LoadHurdleModel(loads; family = :lognormal, lod = 0, scale = :continuous)

Likelihood of swab loads under a two-part (hurdle) model with left-censoring at a limit of detection lod:

  • each swab comes from an infected host with probability π;
  • an infected host's load follows family: :lognormal, :gamma, :weibull or :negbinomial;
  • a load below lod (and any zero) is a non-detect, with probability (1 − π) + π P(Y < lod); a detected load y contributes π f(y).

With lod = 0 zeros are the uninfected and the model is the classic hurdle model. loads are numbers on one basis (state it: ITS1 copies or ZE per swab) or a vector of PathogenLoads of one type.

scale = :count rounds loads up to integers and uses the probability of each integer for every family, so that the negative binomial (which requires it) can be compared with the continuous families by AIC. On the default continuous scale only the continuous families are comparable with each other.

It implements the LogDensityProblems interface: the log density is the log-likelihood at unconstrained parameters (a flat prior) in the order of parameter_names. Fit it with maximum_likelihood; pointwise_loglikelihood gives one term per swab. hurdle_distribution returns the fitted π and distribution.

Chytridiomycosis.fit_positive_loads — Function
fit_positive_loads(loads, family) -> (; family, distribution, loglik, aic)

Maximum likelihood fit of family (:lognormal, :gamma, :weibull or :exponential) to the positive loads in loads (zeros dropped), as Schrock et al. (2025) fitted the Bd-positive swabs of each group. Lognormal and exponential are closed-form; gamma and Weibull are fitted by Nelder–Mead on log parameters. aic = 2k − 2 log L, with k = 1 for the exponential and 2 otherwise.

Fit each family to the positive loads with fit_positive_loads and return the fits sorted by AIC, each with ΔAIC from the best. Schrock et al. (2025) counted a family as fitting a group as well as the best when ΔAIC ≤ 2.

Taylor's power law

Schrock et al. (2025; preprint 2024) related the log variance to the log mean of Bd loads across groups of swabs (species × life stage × site × season × year), zeros included, and found slopes of 1.90 (Sierra Nevada) to 2.06 (Brazil), steeper than the macroparasite 1.55. TaylorPowerLawProblem follows their code: OLS of log10 variance on log10 mean, groups with at least 10 swabs and 3 positives, R's confint intervals.

A lognormal load with the same log-scale spread in every group gives a slope of exactly 2 when every swab is positive. Their groups include the uninfected swabs, so with prevalence π, Var/mean² = (e^{σ²} − π)/π (load_moments): groups whose prevalence rises with their mean load pull the slope below 2.

Their group summaries (Dryad, CC0) are bundled in data/schrock2024/. load_schrock2024 reads them and schrock_taylor_slopes reproduces the published slopes: Sierra Nevada 1.901 (paper 1.90), Brazil 2.055 (2.06), adults 2.030 (2.03), larvae 1.854 (1.85), subadults 1.933 (1.93). The enzootic Sierra slope is 1.98, not the published 2.08, so the claimed difference from post-invasion populations (1.93) is not reproduced; the authors' phase script is not in their code deposit. schrock_family_fractions recomputes their distribution comparison from the AICs they deposited: the lognormal is best or within 2 AIC in 76.1% of 524 groups (paper 76.7% of 525), and the fractions where several families tie (57.3%) or one is clearly best (lognormal 38.0%) match the printed values.

Chytridiomycosis.TaylorPowerLawProblem — Type
TaylorPowerLawProblem(means, variances; base = 10)
TaylorPowerLawProblem(groups; min_swabs = 10, min_infected = 3, base = 10)

Taylor's power law Var = a · mean^b across groups, fitted by ordinary least squares of log(variance) on log(mean) (logs to base).

The second form takes one vector of swab loads per group (zeros for uninfected swabs) and summarises each group as Schrock et al. (2025) did: mean and sample variance over all swabs including the zeros, keeping groups with at least min_swabs swabs and at least min_infected positive swabs. Their thresholds are in the compendium (parameter_set(:schrock2024)).

solve(prob; level = 0.95) returns a NamedTuple: slope (b), intercept (log a), a, their standard errors se_slope, se_intercept, the level confidence interval of the slope ci_slope (t quantiles, as R's confint), n (groups), r2 and sigma (residual standard deviation).

A lognormal load with the same log-scale spread in every group, and every swab infected, gives b = 2 exactly. Groups that differ in prevalence do not: with the zeros included, Var/mean² = (e^{σ²} − π)/π grows as the infected fraction π falls (load_moments).

source
Chytridiomycosis.load_moments — Function
load_moments(prevalence, d::UnivariateDistribution) -> (; mean, variance)

Mean and variance of the load of a randomly chosen swab when a fraction prevalence of hosts is infected with loads distributed as d and the rest read zero: mean = π m, variance = π(v + m²) − π² m², with m and v the mean and variance of d. These are the quantities Taylor's power law relates across groups (Schrock et al. 2025 include the zeros). For a lognormal d with log-scale sd σ, variance/mean² = (e^{σ²} − π)/π.

source
load_moments(d::SwabLoadDistribution) -> (; mean, variance)

Mean and variance of the reported swab load (ZE per swab), with uninfected hosts and non-detects read as zero: the quantities of Taylor's power law (TaylorPowerLawProblem). Uniform components are integrated with a 16-point midpoint rule.

Chytridiomycosis.schrock_family_fractions — Function
schrock_family_fractions(path = schrock2024_path(); min_infected = 10)

Schrock et al.'s (2025) comparison of the continuous load distributions, recomputed from the AIC values they deposited (gamma_aic, lnorm_aic, weibull_aic, exp_aic in the Dryad group summaries), following their 05_Fig.2_Distribution_Fit_Boxplot.R: groups with at least min_infected positive swabs and all four AICs, ΔAIC from the lowest of the four, and "as good" meaning ΔAIC ≤ 2. Returns a named tuple with

  • n: the number of groups;
  • as_good: for each family (:gamma, :lognormal, :weibull, :exponential), the fraction of groups where it is best or within 2 AIC of the best;
  • several: the fraction of groups where more than one family is within 2 AIC;
  • clearly_best: for each family, the fraction where it beats every other by more than 2.

These are the authors' own fits; compare_load_families repeats the fits on raw loads.

Observation operators

swab_distribution turns a model state into a SwabLoadDistribution: a prevalence, a mixture over log10 ZE per swab, optional Gaussian noise σ on the log10 scale and an optional limit of detection. Its swab_logpdf is a density on the natural scale, so it can be compared with the LoadHurdleModel likelihoods, and load_moments gives the mean and variance that Taylor's power law relates.

d = swab_distribution(sol)                    # the published state, as swabs

Field swabs from Knapp et al. (2022) are ITS1 copies, not ZE: compare them with a ZE prediction only after convert_load(x, ZEPerSwab; copies_per_zoospore = …) with a stated copy number.

Chytridiomycosis.SwabLoadDistribution — Type
SwabLoadDistribution(prevalence, lower, upper, weights; σ = 0, lod = 0)

The distribution of the load reported by one swab, in ZE per swab:

  • with probability 1 − prevalence the host is uninfected and the swab reads zero;
  • otherwise its log10 load is drawn from a mixture: component k (weight weights[k]) is uniform on [lower[k], upper[k]] in log10 ZE (a single point when they are equal), plus Gaussian noise of standard deviation σ on the log10 scale;
  • a swab below the limit of detection lod (ZE per swab) reads as a non-detect.

Built by swab_distribution from a model state. Use detection_probability, swab_logpdf, swab_loglikelihood, load_moments and rand(rng, d, n) on it.

Chytridiomycosis.swab_distribution — Function
swab_distribution(loads::AbstractVector{ZEPerSwab}; σ = 0, lod = 0)
swab_distribution(loads::AbstractVector{SporangiaPerHost}; ze_per_sporangium, σ = 0, lod = 0)
swab_distribution(mean_load::ZEPerSwab; sdlog10, prevalence = 1, lod = 0)

swab_distribution(sol::HybridIPMSolution; step = length(sol.t), σ = 0, lod = 0)
swab_distribution(p::LoucaParameters, out; stage = :adult, step = nothing, σ = 0, lod = 0)
    -> SwabLoadDistribution

Observation operators: the distribution of one field swab's reading (ZE per swab) predicted from a model's load state. σ is extra Gaussian noise on the log10 scale and lod a limit of detection (ZE per swab); both default to none.

  • Host loads in ZE per swab (for example a stochastic or individual-based model): each host is a point component; hosts with zero load are uninfected.
  • Sporangia per host (Briggs et al. 2010): no published conversion exists, so this throws an ArgumentError unless ze_per_sporangium is given, and warns when it is. Pass the loads of the living frogs.
  • A mean load (for a deterministic model with no spread among hosts): a lognormal among infected hosts with that arithmetic mean and log10 standard deviation sdlog10. The spread is an assumption of ours, not of any of the published models.
  • Wilber et al. (2016) IPM (BdIPMSolution): the state at step (an index into sol.t); each mesh bin of ln(ZE per swab) is a uniform component (converted to log10), prevalence = infected/(infected + uninfected). The published x is the swab load, so with σ = 0, lod = 0 this only re-expresses the published state.
  • Wilber et al. (2017) hybrid IPM (HybridIPMSolution): adults only (tadpoles carry no load distribution in that model).
  • Louca et al. (2014): out is the result of solve(::LoucaDOIProblem). The final distribution of stage (:adult or :tadpole), or with step a column of PA_t/PT_t (needs save_distributions = true). Bin 1 (DOI 0) is uninfected; bin k is a point at DOI (k − 1)w, read as ZE per swab (ESM §5.1). Point components need σ > 0 for swab_logpdf of a detected swab.

detection_probability(d::SwabLoadDistribution) -> Float64

Probability that a swab is positive (at or above the limit of detection): the prevalence times the fraction of infected hosts' swabs at or above lod.

source
Chytridiomycosis.swab_logpdf — Function
swab_logpdf(d::SwabLoadDistribution, y) -> Float64

Log-likelihood of one swab reading y (ZE per swab, a number or a ZEPerSwab). A zero or a reading below d.lod is a non-detect, with log probability log(1 − detection_probability(d)); a positive reading contributes the density of y on the natural scale (the log10 density divided by y ln 10), so likelihoods are comparable with those of LoadHurdleModel on the continuous scale. Point components need σ > 0.

Validation

The registry block # --- observation load of data/validation/targets.toml holds the reproduced numbers (Longo et al.'s efficiencies, copy-number range and amplicon mass; the slope of 2 for lognormal loads; Maguire et al.'s 80% viability) and two discrepancies: Longo et al.'s text gives 6.0 × 10⁶ copies in 1.5 ng where Fig. 4B's highest bar reads 6.4 × 10⁶, and Chestnut et al.'s "approximately four orders of magnitude" between their largest filter and any previous observation is 2.81 orders against the Nevada filter they name.
