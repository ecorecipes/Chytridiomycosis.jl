Pradel recruitment and growth (Hardy 2023)

This is a reference implementation of the capture–recapture analysis of Hardy et al. (2023), J Appl Ecol 60:121–131 (doi:10.1111/1365-2664.14310): a robust-design Pradel model of adult male boreal toads (Anaxyrus boreas boreas) at five Colorado sites, 1998–2020, with Bd arrival as a before/after covariate. The data are the authors' Dryad deposit (doi:10.5061/dryad.fttdz08wv, CC0), bundled in data/hardy2023.

Version read

Equations and numbers were checked against the version of record and its Supporting Information (Appendices S1.1–S1.6). They were first read from the reprint in Chapter 1 of B. M. Hardy's PhD dissertation (Colorado State University, 2023), which prints the same numbers; only its table and figure numbers differ.

The fit reproduces:

  • the capture counts of Table 1 and the 1,594 marked toads;
  • 26 of the 27 focal models of Appendices S1.3–S1.5 to 0.01 in −2 log L (and their K and AICc);
  • the post-Bd survival (0), recruitment (0.25, SE 0.02) and growth rate (0.25, 95% CI 0.21–0.30);
  • the range of capture probabilities (0.03–0.81) and the pre-Bd survival at Morgan's Gulch (0.83, SE 0.01).

Nine targets are discrepant. Our fit of φ(t^Bd) is 9.28 units of −2 log L better than MARK's. The top model's AICc is 26649.35 against 26649.10. The printed −2 log L of Table 3 (10503.97) does not go with its own AICc and K. The second model of Table 3 is 48.45 AICc units behind, not 56.81. The pre-Bd λ range (0.69–1.40 against 0.65–1.46), the elevation coefficients (0.27 and −0.11 against 0.28 and −0.12) and the survival at Delilah Pond (0.48 against 0.47) differ by less than one printed SE. See the hardy2023_* targets.

Muths, Scherer & Pilliod (2011, J Appl Ecol, doi:10.1111/j.1365-2664.2011.02005.x) used the same reverse-time model on two boreal toad populations and suggested compensatory recruitment after Bd; Hardy et al. test that idea. Its data are not deposited, and it is not implemented here.

The Pradel temporal-symmetry model

Pradel (1996) models a population forward in time with apparent survival φ and backward in time with seniority γ. In the f parameterization the per-capita recruitment is f, the realized growth rate is λ = φ + f, and γ_{t+1} = φ_t / λ_t. In the robust design, each primary period (year) has several secondary occasions with capture probability p (here p = c), and p* = 1 − ∏(1 − p). The likelihood is conditional on the number of animals ever caught, and abundance is derived as N = n / p*. The layer is general (any number of groups, any design matrices, standard or robust design). It reproduces Program MARK's −2 log L exactly.

Chytridiomycosis.PradelGroup — Type
PradelGroup(histories; primary = 1:size(histories, 2))

Capture histories of one group (for example one site) for the Pradel (1996) temporal-symmetry model.

  • histories is an n×J matrix with one row per animal and one column per secondary occasion: 1 caught, 0 not caught, missing (or -1) for an occasion that was not sampled. The sampling pattern must be the same in every row.
  • primary[j] is the primary period (1…T) of column j. The default, one occasion per period, gives the standard Pradel model; several columns per period give the robust design with Huggins closed captures and p = c, as in Program MARK.

Rows with no capture are ignored. The constructor reduces the histories to the counts that the likelihood needs; see pradel_loglikelihood.

Chytridiomycosis.pradel_loglikelihood — Function
pradel_loglikelihood(g::PradelGroup, φ, f, p) -> Real

Log-likelihood of the Pradel (1996) temporal-symmetry model with apparent survival φ and per-capita recruitment f (both of length T − 1, for the intervals between primary periods) and capture probabilities p (one per secondary occasion), conditional on the number of animals ever caught. With several occasions per period it is the robust-design Pradel model of Program MARK (Huggins closed captures, p = c). The realized population growth rate is λ = φ + f.

On the boreal toad data of Hardy et al. (2023) it reproduces MARK's −2 log L (for example 29334.27 for the constant model; see test/hardy2023.jl).

Chytridiomycosis.pradel_capture_probability — Function
pradel_capture_probability(g::PradelGroup, p) -> Vector

The probability p*_t = 1 − ∏_j (1 − p_tj) of catching an animal present in period t at least once, over the sampled occasions of each period (zero for a period that was not sampled). p has one entry per occasion (column of the histories).

Chytridiomycosis.pradel_abundance — Function
pradel_abundance(g::PradelGroup, p) -> Vector

Abundance in each primary period estimated as N_t = n_t / p*_t, where n_t is the number of animals caught in period t (Huggins estimator, as derived by MARK's robust-design Pradel model). NaN for a period that was not sampled.

Chytridiomycosis.PradelProblem — Type
PradelProblem(groups, designs; names = nothing)

Maximum-likelihood problem for a multi-group Pradel model with linear predictors.

  • groups is a vector of PradelGroups.
  • designs[g] is a named tuple (φ = Xφ, f = Xf, p = Xp) of design matrices for group g: Xφ and Xf have T − 1 rows (intervals), Xp one row per occasion. All groups share the coefficient vector θ = [βφ; βf; βp] (the matrices of one parameter must have the same number of columns in every group).
  • Links: logit φ = Xφ βφ, log f = Xf βf, logit p = Xp βp, as in Hardy et al. (2023, Appendix S1.2). Rows of Xp for unsampled occasions are ignored.

names optionally labels the coefficients (default φ_1, …, f_1, …, p_1, …). The problem implements the LogDensityProblems interface (log density = log-likelihood, flat prior on θ). solve(prob) returns a PradelFit.

source
Chytridiomycosis.PradelFit — Type
PradelFit

Maximum-likelihood fit of a PradelProblem from solve(prob). Fields:

  • problem, θ (coefficients, in the order of problem.names), vcov (inverse observed information of θ; coefficients with no curvature, such as an estimate on a boundary, are left out and get NaN; all NaN if the rest is singular) and gradient (at the optimum);
  • loglik, deviance (−2 log L, MARK's "−2log(L)"), k (number of coefficients), n (nobs(prob), the total number of captures) and aicc;
  • groups: for each group a named tuple of φ, f, λ = φ + f (intervals), p (occasions), pstar and N (periods; N = n_t / p*_t).

Standard errors of derived quantities: pradel_se.

Chytridiomycosis.pradel_se — Function
pradel_se(fit::PradelFit, quantity::Function) -> (value, se)

Value and delta-method standard error of a scalar function quantity(θ) of the coefficients of a PradelFit, with a central-difference gradient. Coefficients on which the quantity does not depend (zero gradient) are skipped, so a boundary estimate elsewhere does not make the result NaN. For example the realized growth rate of group 2 in interval 5: pradel_se(fit, θ -> pradel_derived(fit.problem, θ, 2).λ[5]).

Chytridiomycosis.simulate_pradel — Function
simulate_pradel(rng, N1, φ, f, p; primary = 1:length(p)) -> Matrix{Int}

Simulate capture histories under the Pradel model. N1 animals are present in period 1; between periods t and t + 1 each survives with probability φ[t] and Poisson(f[t] N_t) recruits enter. Each animal present in period t is caught at each secondary occasion j (with primary[j] = t) with probability p[j]. Returns the histories of the animals caught at least once (rows) by occasion (columns).

Boreal toads in Colorado (Hardy et al. 2023)

Two choices were made where the paper is silent. Both are checked by the printed −2 log L.

  • An interval t → t + 1 takes the Bd status of year t.
  • The climate covariates for recruitment are those of three years before the recruits are counted.

The drought and winter-onset columns of two of the deposited covariate files carry swapped headers. The loader assigns them by value.

Hardy2023Site

One boreal toad site of Hardy et al. (2023), from load_hardy2023:

Chytridiomycosis.hardy2023_problem — Function
hardy2023_problem(; φ = :elev_t_bd, f = :elev_t_bd, p = :s_bd_t_bd,
                  data = load_hardy2023(), bd_interval = :start) -> PradelProblem

The robust-design Pradel model of Hardy et al. (2023) for the five boreal toad sites (one group per site) as a PradelProblem. The defaults are the paper's top model, p(s^Bd × t^Bd) φ(ELEV + t^Bd) f(ELEV + t^Bd) (Table 3).

Structures (Appendices S1.3–S1.5):

  • p: :constant, :site, :t_bd (year before Bd), :s_bd (site before Bd), :s_bd_t_bd (site × year before Bd). With _bd the post-Bd value is one parameter for all sites.
  • φ: :constant, :elev, :bd, :t_bd, :elev_t_bd, :elev_bd, :elev_x_bd.
  • f: those of φ and :drought, :winstart, :drought_bd, :drought_x_bd, :winstart_bd, :winstart_x_bd, :drought_elev, :winstart_elev (_bd additive, _x_bd interaction; covariates of the year three years before the recruits are counted).

bd_interval sets which year's Bd status an interval t → t + 1 takes (:start, year t, the default, or :end). Coefficient names are in prob.names.

Chytridiomycosis.hardy2023_summary — Function
hardy2023_summary(fit::PradelFit; data = load_hardy2023()) -> NamedTuple

The quantities that Hardy et al. (2023) report from a fit of hardy2023_problem:

  • φ_post, f_post, λ_post: the post-Bd apparent survival, per-capita recruitment and realized growth rate (shared by all sites), each (value, se); λ_post also has a 95% interval ci on the log scale;
  • λ_pre: the realized growth rates (site, year, value, se) of the intervals year → year + 1 that end before Bd arrival (Fig. 3), between sampled years of each site;
  • λ_pre_range: the smallest and largest of those;
  • N: the estimated number of breeding males of each site by year (Fig. 2), NaN in unsampled years;
  • p_range: the range of the estimated capture probabilities at sampled occasions.

φ_post, f_post and λ_post are taken from the post-Bd interval of the site with the earliest arrival.
