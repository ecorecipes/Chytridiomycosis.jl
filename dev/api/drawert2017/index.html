Stochastic individual-based model (Drawert 2017)

This is a reference implementation of the model of Drawert, Griesemer, Petzold and Briggs (2017, J R Soc Interface 14:20170480, 10.1098/rsif.2017.0480). It takes the Briggs et al. (2010) load model and makes it individual-based and demographically stochastic: each frog carries a whole number of sporangia, and the pond holds a whole number of zoospores. The paper asks which one-time intervention best helps a frog population survive the first Bd outbreak: treating frogs with antifungals, culling frogs, or disinfecting the pond.

It was checked against the paper, its supplementary text and the authors' C code and parameter table (figshare 10.6084/m9.figshare.5328127, CC BY 4.0). Time is in days.

The model

For each live frog i with load S_i, and the zoospores Z in a pool of volume 1, the deterministic version is (eqs 3.1–3.2)

\[\frac{dS_i}{dt} = \nu(S_i)\,\gamma Z + \nu(S_i)\,\eta f S_i - \sigma(S_i) S_i, \qquad \frac{dZ}{dt} = \sum_i \bigl[\eta(1-f) S_i - \gamma Z\bigr] - \mu Z .\]

A frog dies when its load exceeds Smax = 10 000, and its sporangia die with it.

  • Zoospores meet each frog at rate γ and encyst with probability ν(S).
  • Sporangia release η zoospores a day. A fraction f of them meet their own frog again, and the rest go to the pool.
  • Sporangia are lost at rate σ(S), and zoospores die at rate μ.

There are three variants:

  • Baseline: ν = ν0 and σ = σ0. These are the Briggs et al. (2010) equations.
  • Nu: encystment falls with load, ν(S) = ν0 exp(−ν1 S).
  • Sigma: sporangium loss rises with load, σ(S) = σ0 + σ1 S.

In the Nu and Sigma variants, ν1 or σ1 is chosen so that loads would level off at S* × Smax, with S* = 1.2 or 2 (just above the lethal load).

The stochastic version (ESM Table S3) turns each term into an event: a zoospore encysting, a sporangium reinfecting its own frog, a zoospore being released, a sporangium being lost, a zoospore dying. DrawertIBMProblem simulates it exactly, event by event, as the authors' C code does. It starts with one frog carrying 100 sporangia, every other frog uninfected and an empty pool. A run ends in one of three ways:

  • frog extinction: all frogs have died;
  • fungal extinction: no sporangia or zoospores are left;
  • persistence: frogs and fungus are both present after 1000 days.

The paper samples 2132 parameter points over wide ranges (Table 1). It kept only points where Bd grows at 0.035–0.35 per day, so that every frog dies in the deterministic model, and ran each point 30 times. It then sorted the points into five groups by the mean and spread of the fraction of frogs that survive (Fig. 3):

  • A: all frogs always die;
  • B: almost all die;
  • C: highly variable outcomes;
  • D: most survive;
  • E: partial die-offs.

The authors' table of points and results is bundled (load_drawert2017).

Chytridiomycosis.DrawertParameters — Type
DrawertParameters(; N, γ, η, ν0, f, σ0, μ, ν1 = 0, σ1 = 0, Smax, S0, Z0 = 0, V = 1)
DrawertParameters(:drawert2017; set = "", point = nothing, kwargs...)

Parameters of the Drawert et al. (2017) model. Rates are per day.

  • N: initial number of frogs;
  • γ: encounter rate between a zoospore and a frog (frog⁻¹ day⁻¹, for a pool of volume V);
  • η: zoospores released per sporangium per day;
  • ν0: probability that a zoospore encysts when it meets an uninfected frog;
  • f: fraction of released zoospores that meet their own frog again;
  • σ0: sporangium loss rate;
  • μ: zoospore death rate in the pool;
  • ν1: decline of encystment with load, ν(S) = ν0 exp(−ν1 S) (the Nu model);
  • σ1: increase of sporangium loss with load, σ(S) = σ0 + σ1 S (the Sigma model);
  • Smax: lethal load (sporangia); a frog dies when its load would exceed it;
  • S0: sporangia on the one frog infected at time 0; Z0: zoospores in the pool then;
  • V: pool volume (1 in the paper).

ν1 = σ1 = 0 is the Baseline model. See drawert_variant for the Nu and Sigma models. BriggsParameters(p) gives the BriggsParameters with the same Baseline dynamics (ν = ν0, σ = σ0, the same V, γ, f, μ, η and Smax): with ν1 = σ1 = 0 the deterministic model is the Briggs et al. (2010) model.

The compendium form reads Smax, S0, Z0 and V from data/compendium/drawert2017.toml:

  • with set = "groupA" … "groupE", the biological parameters are the representative point of that group (ESM Table S9, as printed);
  • with point = id, they are point id of the authors' table of 2132 sampled points (load_drawert2017), at full precision.

Keyword arguments override single values.

Chytridiomycosis.drawert_variant — Function
drawert_variant(p, variant; S_star, as_printed = false) -> DrawertParameters

The Baseline point p turned into the :nu or :sigma model (or back to :baseline), with the load dependence chosen so that loads level off at S_star × Smax (ESM §1.4; the paper uses S_star = 1.2 and 2, which the compendium holds as S_star_low, S_star_high):

  • :nu: ν1 = ln(ν0 K/σ0)/(S* Smax), the inverse of the S* formula of ESM §1.1.2;
  • :sigma: σ1 = (ν0 K − σ0)/(S* Smax), ESM eq. (12);

with K as in equilibrium_load_ratio. ESM eq. (11) prints ν1 = ν0 γη(1 − f)N/(σ0 (γN + μ))/(S* Smax), without the logarithm and the ηf term; as_printed = true uses it. The printed ν1 does not give loads that level off at S* × Smax.

source
Chytridiomycosis.equilibrium_load_ratio — Function
equilibrium_load_ratio(p::DrawertParameters) -> S*

The load at which Bd growth on each frog stops, as a multiple of Smax, for N frogs with equal loads and the pool at quasi-steady state (ESM §1.1.2, §1.1.3). With K = γη(1 − f)N/(γN + μV) + ηf:

  • Nu model: S* = ln(ν0 K/σ0)/(ν1 Smax);
  • Sigma model: S* = (ν0 K − σ0)/(σ1 Smax);
  • Baseline: Inf.

Frogs die in the deterministic model when S* > 1.

Chytridiomycosis.load_growth_rate — Method
load_growth_rate(p::DrawertParameters) -> λ

The linearised Bd growth rate of the Baseline model with p.N frogs: the dominant eigenvalue of the linear system (ESM §1.1.1, §1.4), the same as load_growth_rate(BriggsParameters(p), p.N). The paper kept points with 0.035 ≤ λ ≤ 0.35 day⁻¹.

Chytridiomycosis.nondimensional_parameters — Function
nondimensional_parameters(p::DrawertParameters) -> (; c1, c2, c3, c4)

The non-dimensional groups of Table 2 (ESM eqs 3–6), with γ read as γ/V:

  • c1 = η ν0 f / σ0: self-reinfection;

  • c3 = γ N / σ0: zoospore loss by meeting frogs;
  • c4 = μ / σ0: background zoospore loss.

nondimensional_ranges(cs) -> (; c1, c2, c3, c4)

The ranges used to scale nondimensional_distance, from a collection cs of (c1, c2, c3, c4) named tuples: max − min of c1, log c2, log c3 and c4. With as_printed = true the c2 and c3 entries are max − min of c2 and c3 themselves.

Chytridiomycosis.nondimensional_distance — Function
nondimensional_distance(a, b, ranges; as_printed = false)

The distance between two points of non-dimensional parameter space (ESM eq. 10), each a (c1, c2, c3, c4) named tuple:

d = √(Δc1²/r1² + Δlog c2²/r2² + Δlog c3²/r3² + Δc4²/r4²)

ranges comes from nondimensional_ranges. By default r2 and r3 are the ranges of log c2 and log c3, which is how the authors' points are spaced: no two of the 2132 are closer than 0.0901, just above the threshold ε = 0.09. Eq. (10) prints log(c2range), the log of the range of c2; as_printed = true uses that (pass ranges made with as_printed = true).

Chytridiomycosis.DrawertIBMProblem — Type
DrawertIBMProblem(p; intervention = nothing, tfinal = 1000, saveat = Float64[],
                  save_loads = false, as_coded = false)

The stochastic individual-based model of Drawert et al. (2017) (ESM §1.3, Table S3): an integer load on each frog and an integer number of zoospores in the pool, simulated exactly with the direct method (Gillespie). One frog starts with p.S0 sporangia, the others with none, and the pool with p.Z0 zoospores. tfinal (1000 days) comes from the compendium.

intervention is a DrawertIntervention or nothing. With as_coded = true, cleaning the environment removes every zoospore, as the authors' C code does (the paper describes removing the given fraction).

solve(prob; rng) returns a named tuple:

  • outcome: :frogs_extinct, :fungus_extinct (no sporangia or zoospores left, some frogs alive) or :persistence (both present at tfinal);
  • survivors, surviving_fraction (survivors / N; culled frogs do not survive);
  • death_time: per frog, the time it died of chytridiomycosis (NaN otherwise); culled: per frog, whether it was culled;
  • first_loss: the first time a frog died or was culled (Inf if none);
  • t_end: when the outcome was settled (or tfinal); events: the number of reactions;
  • t, frogs, zoospores, sporangia: the state at the saveat times, and, with save_loads = true, loads, an N × length(saveat) matrix (-1 for a dead frog).

The run stops when its outcome is settled unless later saveat times remain.

source
Chytridiomycosis.DrawertODEProblem — Type
DrawertODEProblem(p; intervention = nothing, tfinal = 1000)

The deterministic version of the model (eqs 3.1–3.2; ESM eqs 1–2): one load per frog and the pool, from the same initial state as DrawertIBMProblem. A frog dies (its load is removed) when it reaches Smax.

Interventions follow the authors' determ_frogs.c, which the paper does not describe:

  • cleaning and culling act on floor(fraction × N) live frogs, at least one, taken in index order (frog 1 is the one infected first);
  • culling does nothing when one frog or none is left;
  • cleaning the environment multiplies Z by 1 − fraction.

solve(prob; kwargs...) integrates with Tsit5 and returns (; sol, outcome, survivors, surviving_fraction, death_time, t_end). The outcome is as for the stochastic model: :fungus_extinct when, on some whole day, the total load and Z are both below 1e-6, as in the authors' code. to_ode_problem(prob) gives the ODEProblem.

Interventions

Each intervention is a single pulse on day 14, 28 or 56, treating 25%, 50% or 75% (§3.3):

  • cleaning frogs clears all sporangia from a random set of frogs;
  • culling removes a random set of frogs;
  • cleaning the environment removes zoospores from the pool.

The number of frogs (or zoospores) treated is binomial. The paper's Fig. 4 counts the fraction of points at which the mean surviving fraction rises (or falls) by at least 0.1, 0.3 or 0.5. The paper's main finding is that cleaning frogs helps at 20–30% of points and rarely harms, culling is riskier, and cleaning the environment does little.

The authors' code removes every zoospore when it cleans the environment, whatever the fraction (clean_env() sets Z = 0 before drawing the binomial). This fits the paper's remark that this treatment is insensitive to the fraction removed. as_coded = true reproduces the code; the default follows the paper's description.

Chytridiomycosis.DrawertIntervention — Type
DrawertIntervention(kind, day, fraction)

A one-time intervention (§3.3), applied at time day:

  • :clean_frogs: Binomial(N, fraction) live frogs, chosen at random, are cleared of all sporangia (antifungal treatment);
  • :cull_frogs: Binomial(N, fraction) live frogs, chosen at random, are removed;
  • :clean_environment: Binomial(Z, fraction) zoospores are removed from the pool (all of them with as_coded = true in DrawertIBMProblem).

N and Z are the live frogs and pool zoospores at day.

What reproduces

The heavy runs are scripts/drawert2017_*.jl. Their output is stored in data/drawert2017/reproduction.toml, which the tests and validation targets read.

  • Deterministic model, all 2132 points. λ equals the authors' column (to 1e-6).
  • Deterministic extinction. Every point ends in frog extinction, in the Baseline and in the four Nu/Sigma variants, "by construction" as the paper says.
  • Stochastic Baseline, 116 points. We drew a stratified random sample of points and ran each 30 times, then compared it with the authors' 30 runs:
    • 113/116 points have the same Fig. 2a outcome class and 105/116 the same Fig. 3 group;
    • no Fisher test on frog-extinction counts rejects, and 5/116 Welch tests on the mean surviving fraction reject at 5%;
    • the weighted fractions are 65.4% frog extinction (paper 65%), 24.2% fungal extinction (24%), 10.4% variable (~10%) and 65.4% in Group A (65%).
  • Nu and Sigma. Load regulation lets frogs and fungus coexist (18–32% of runs at S* = 1.2 on a random 12-point sample, against none in the Baseline).
  • Interventions (Fig. 4, Fig. S14), on 66 sample points and two full 27-strategy grids:
    • cleaning frogs never harms, and helps at most Group B/C points;
    • culling helps and harms in Groups B/C, and harms in Group D, more when larger and earlier;
    • cleaning the environment changes outcomes no more often than a placebo (a second no-intervention ensemble).
  • Not reproduced. The overall Fig. 4 bars and Fig. 2a's Nu/Sigma bars need the full design.

Three printed statements disagree with the authors' own data or with the rest of the paper:

  • ESM eq. (11) for ν1 lacks the logarithm and the ηf term of the S* formula it should invert. With the printed form, frogs would survive deterministically at 270 points. drawert_variant(p, :nu; as_printed = true) uses it.
  • "Death of all frogs within 14–275 days" (§3.2) fits the day of the first death: rounded up to whole days, it spans exactly 14–275. The last death comes as late as day 567.
  • "Coexisting in about 0.01% of the cases": the authors' table has 3 of 2132 points (0.14%).

ESM eq. (10) also prints log(c_range) where the authors' points are spaced by the range of log c (no two closer than 0.0901, with ε = 0.09).
