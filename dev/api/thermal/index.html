Thermal performance

Temperature-dependent rates for the mechanistic models. A curve maps temperature (°C) to a rate. IsolateTPC and HostTPC attach a curve to a pathogen isolate or a host trait, with its provenance. SeasonalTemperature and temperature_dependent turn a curve into a time-varying rate.

Published thermal data are in the compendium:

  • Cardinal temperatures. Limits and optima from Stevenson et al. 2013 and Sheets et al. 2021 (Bd), and Martel et al. 2013 (Bsal). There are no fitted curves.
  • Kelly et al. 2024. Fitted growth-rate polynomials for 13 Bsal isolates (IsolateTPC(:kelly2024, "Eifel2019")).
  • Woodhams et al. 2008. The Bd life-history rates at several temperatures.
  • Blooi et al. 2015. Heat clearance of Bsal (25 °C for 10 days).

A curve built from cardinal temperatures, such as a Briere through Martel's values, rests on the choice of family and is not a published curve.

Curves

Chytridiomycosis.ThermalPerformanceCurve — Type
ThermalPerformanceCurve

Supertype of the thermal performance curves. Every curve c is callable as c(T) with T in °C, and returns a rate in the units of whatever it was built from. The curves are generic in the number type, so they work with automatic differentiation.

Families: Briere, CardinalTPC, GaussianQuadratic, SharpeSchoolfield, Ratkowsky, PolynomialTPC and InterpolatedTPC. Use thermal_optimum to locate a curve's peak.

source
Chytridiomycosis.Briere — Type
Briere(; a, Tmin, Tmax, m = 2)
Briere(; Tmin, Topt, Tmax, rmax)

The Brière curve (Brière et al. 1999), r(T) = a T (T − Tmin) (Tmax − T)^(1/m), for Tmin < T < Tmax and zero outside. m = 2 is Brière-1, and a free m is Brière-2. It is left-skewed, with an abrupt fall near Tmax.

The second form takes the cardinal temperatures and the peak rate, which is what most chytrid papers report. The optimum satisfies 1/m = (Tmax − Topt)(1/Topt + 1/(Topt − Tmin)), which fixes m, and then a gives r(Topt) = rmax. It needs 0 ≤ Tmin < Topt < Tmax.

source
Chytridiomycosis.CardinalTPC — Type
CardinalTPC(; Tmin, Topt, Tmax, rmax)

The cardinal temperature model with inflection (Rosso et al. 1993). It is set directly by the three cardinal temperatures and the rate at the optimum:

r(T) = rmax (T − Tmax)(T − Tmin)² /
       ((Topt − Tmin)[(Topt − Tmin)(T − Topt) − (Topt − Tmax)(Topt + Tmin − 2T)])

for Tmin < T < Tmax, and zero outside. Rosso et al. require Topt > (Tmin + Tmax)/2, and the constructor enforces it. Bsal's reported cardinal temperatures (optimum 10–15 °C, growth from 5 to 22 °C; Martel et al. 2013) fall outside that domain; use Briere for them.

source
Chytridiomycosis.GaussianQuadratic — Type
GaussianQuadratic(; rmax, Topt, σ, CTmax)

A Gaussian rise and a quadratic fall (Deutsch et al. 2008): rmax exp(−((T − Topt)/(2σ))²) for T ≤ Topt, and rmax (1 − ((T − Topt)/(Topt − CTmax))²) for Topt < T < CTmax. It is zero from CTmax up. The curve is asymmetric, with a long cool tail and an abrupt upper decline.

source
Chytridiomycosis.SharpeSchoolfield — Type
SharpeSchoolfield(; r_ref, E, Eh, Th, El = nothing, Tl = nothing, Tref = 20)

The Sharpe–Schoolfield curve (Schoolfield, Sharpe & Magnuson 1981). It is an Arrhenius rise with activation energy E (eV), divided by terms for enzyme inactivation at high, and optionally low, temperatures:

r(T) = r_ref exp(E/k (1/Tref − 1/T)) /
       (1 + exp(Eh/k (1/Th − 1/T)) + exp(El/k (1/T − 1/Tl)))

Temperatures are given in °C and converted to kelvin, and k is Boltzmann's constant in eV K⁻¹. Eh, El > 0, and each inactivation term equals 1 at its half-inactivation temperature Th or Tl. Leave out El and Tl to drop low-temperature inactivation. r_ref is the uninhibited rate at Tref.

source
Chytridiomycosis.Ratkowsky — Type
Ratkowsky(; b, Tmin, c, Tmax)

The Ratkowsky model for microbial growth (Ratkowsky et al. 1983): √r = b (T − Tmin)(1 − exp(c (T − Tmax))) for Tmin < T < Tmax, and zero outside.

source
Chytridiomycosis.PolynomialTPC — Type
PolynomialTPC(coefficients; Tmin = -Inf, Tmax = Inf, nonnegative = true)

A polynomial in temperature, Σ coefficients[k+1] T^k. This is the form Kelly et al. (2024) fitted to the growth rates of each Bsal isolate. Outside [Tmin, Tmax], the range the polynomial was fitted on, it returns zero rather than extrapolating. With nonnegative = true, negative values are set to zero.

source
Chytridiomycosis.InterpolatedTPC — Type
InterpolatedTPC(temperatures, values; extrapolation = :zero)

A rate measured at a few temperatures, interpolated linearly between them. Outside the measured range it is zero (extrapolation = :zero) or the nearest measured value (:flat). Use it when the data are too sparse to choose a family, as with Woodhams et al.'s (2008) zoospore rates at four temperatures (thermal_data(:woodhams2008, :mZ; set_prefix = "table1")).

source
Chytridiomycosis.thermal_optimum — Function
thermal_optimum(c::ThermalPerformanceCurve; range = (-5, 45), step = 0.01) -> Float64

The temperature (°C) at which c peaks within range. It searches a grid of spacing step and then refines with a golden-section search around the best grid point.

source
Chytridiomycosis.thermal_data — Function
thermal_data(key::Symbol, symbol::Symbol; set_prefix = "") -> (temperatures, values)

The values of compendium symbol symbol measured at several temperatures. It reads the records whose system.temperature_C is a number and whose parameter set starts with set_prefix, sorted by temperature. For example, thermal_data(:woodhams2008, :mZ; set_prefix = "table1") gives the encystment rates of Table 1. Pass them to InterpolatedTPC or to a fit.

Isolates, hosts and forcing

Chytridiomycosis.IsolateTPC — Type
IsolateTPC(pathogen, isolate, lineage, trait, curve, source)
IsolateTPC(key::Symbol, isolate::AbstractString)

A thermal performance curve for one trait of one pathogen isolate, with its provenance. It is callable like its curve.

  • pathogen: "Bd" or "Bsal".
  • trait: for example "growth rate r (day⁻¹)".
  • source: the compendium key.

The keyed form builds the curve from the compendium. So far this means IsolateTPC(:kelly2024, "Eifel2019"): Kelly et al.'s (2024) fitted polynomial for that isolate, on the 0–30 °C range they tested.

source
Chytridiomycosis.HostTPC — Type
HostTPC(species, trait, curve, source)

A thermal performance curve for a host trait, such as performance, immune function or survival of infection. It is kept separate from pathogen curves (IsolateTPC) so that thermal mismatch can be built from the two. It is callable like its curve. No host curve has yet been verified for the compendium.

source
Chytridiomycosis.SeasonalTemperature — Type
SeasonalTemperature(; mean, amplitude, peak, period = 365)

A sinusoidal temperature (°C) through the year: T(t) = mean + amplitude cos(2π (t − peak)/period). The phase is stated explicitly as the time peak of the warmest point, in the same time unit as period. Callable as T(t).

source
Chytridiomycosis.temperature_dependent — Function
temperature_dependent(rate, T) -> t -> rate(T(t))

Compose a rate function of temperature (a ThermalPerformanceCurve, an IsolateTPC or any callable) with a temperature series T(t), such as a SeasonalTemperature or an interpolant of observed temperatures. The result is a time-varying rate to use inside an ODE, SDE or jump right-hand side.

Bd life history in culture (Woodhams et al. 2008)

Chytridiomycosis.WoodhamsParameters — Type
WoodhamsParameters(; F, s, dZ, mZ)
WoodhamsParameters(:woodhams2008; set = "23C")

Life-history parameters of Bd in culture (Woodhams et al. 2008, Table 2):

  • F: viable zoospores produced per zoosporangium;
  • s: mean hours to sporulation;
  • dZ: per capita zoospore mortality (h⁻¹);
  • mZ: per capita zoospore encystment (h⁻¹).

The compendium holds Table 2 as two sets, "10C" and "23C". See intrinsic_growth_rate.

source
Chytridiomycosis.intrinsic_growth_rate — Function
intrinsic_growth_rate(p::WoodhamsParameters) -> λ (h⁻¹)

The long-term growth rate of a Bd population in culture: the real root of Woodhams et al.'s (2008) characteristic equation, F mZ exp(−λs) = λ + mZ + dZ. The left side falls and the right side rises in λ, so the root is unique. It is found by bisection and refined by Newton's method.

The paper reports λ = 0.044 h⁻¹ at 23 °C and 0.021 h⁻¹ at 10 °C (validation targets woodhams2008_lambda_23C and woodhams2008_lambda_10C). It is not log(F)/s.
