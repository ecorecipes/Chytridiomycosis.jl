Bsal management decisions with an IPM (Canessa et al. 2018)

Canessa et al. (2018, J Appl Ecol 55:1987–1996, doi:10.1111/1365-2664.13089) asked how, if at all, a B. salamandrivorans outbreak in a fire salamander population could be managed. They compared eight actions over three months, using an integral projection model (IPM) whose functions were elicited from ten experts. Uncertainty was carried through 1,000 random parameter sets. The equations were checked against the review copy of the paper, the only version available (main text, Table 1, Figs 1–4, Supporting Information eqs S1–S12 and Table S1). The 1,000 sets are the authors' Dryad deposit (doi:10.5061/dryad.0jh5p, CC0), bundled as data/canessa2018/parameter_draws.csv.

The model

There are susceptible adults S(t) and infected adults I(z, t) indexed by log-load z = ln(GE). Each step is three days, and there are 30 steps. There is no recruitment, no density dependence and no recovery (eq. S2):

\[\begin{aligned} S(t+1) &= S(t)\,s_0\,\bigl(1-\phi(t)\bigr),\\ I(z',t+1) &= S(t)\,s_0\,\phi(t)\,G_0(z') + \int_l^u I(z,t)\,s(z)\,G(z',z)\,dz,\\ \phi(t) &= 1-\exp\Bigl(-\int_l^u \beta(z)\,I(z,t)\,dz\Bigr). \end{aligned}\]

  • Survival: s(z) = logistic(b₀ + b₁z) (eq. S4).
  • Transmission: β(z) = β₀ logistic(b₂ + b₃z) (eq. S5).
  • Initial load: G₀ = N(ln 200, 0.5) (eq. S7).
  • Growth: z' is normal with mean b₄ + b₅z + b₆z² (eq. S9) and variance σ²_G exp(b₇z) (eq. S10, borrowed from Bd).

The integrals use the midpoint rule on 300 points in [-10, 15]. CanessaIPMProblem iterates the model. Its reproduction number is R₀ = S₀ s₀ ∫ β(z) [(I − P)⁻¹ G₀](z) dz, the infections an adult causes over its infected life (Wilber et al. 2016); basic_reproduction_number(p::CanessaParameters) computes it.

Each of the 1,000 runs refits the functions to one archived draw (CanessaDraw):

  • logistic regressions of five elicited survival and transmission probabilities (loads 0, 10, …, 10⁴ GE, with load 0 at z = −1);
  • a quadratic regression of a Beverton–Holt load series for eq. S9 (canessa_growth_coefficients).

canessa_ensemble runs all the draws. canessa_summary reduces the runs to the medians and 95% intervals of Fig. 2, and canessa_dispersal gives the movement distances of Fig. 3.

What the PDF does not say, and what does not agree

  • Table S1 is not the ensemble. Its coefficients are fits to the experts' best estimates. The draws come from the pooled elicitation and lie far from them. For example, survival at 100 GE is 0.62 in Table S1 against a draw median of 0.895. The Table S1 point model has R₀ ≈ 5.1, while the ensemble median is 9.4.
  • Growth coefficients. The regression behind eq. S9 is only described. Ten Beverton–Holt steps from L₀ = 10, with Lmax = 10⁴ GE and 25 zoospores per zoospore in 5 days, reproduce Table S1's b₄–b₆ to four decimals. These best estimates are recorded in the compendium as inferred (verified = false).
  • The cap at Lmax. The SI formula only sets the expected next load to ln Lmax once the cap is reached. Read literally, half of the loads at the cap then exceed it, where survival is lower, and every R0 quantile is about 14% below the paper's (median 8.2). The default, cap = :truncate, does not let loads exceed Lmax. This reproduces the R0 values of Fig. 2: median 9.43 (95% interval 5.60–14.35) against 9.6 (5.8–14.3). Thinning by 50% gives 4.72 against 4.8, and thinning by 90% gives 0.94 against 0.93. cap = :printed gives the literal reading.
  • Units of β₀ and the final densities. Eq. S6 gives the printed β₀ = 0.007 only when it is solved with the number of adults on Stegen's 475 m × 10 m transect (503 × 0.475 = 238.9) rather than the density 503 ha⁻¹, which gives 0.0039. The archived draws agree: read this way, every (β₀, S₀) pair implies a valid s̄ (mean 0.544, printed 0.542), whereas per hectare 13% would need s̄ < 0. So β₀ is per adult on the transect. Simulating in those units (units = :transect, the default: S₀ × 0.475 susceptibles and one infected adult) reproduces Fig. 2's final densities: 0.951 for action e (plotted 1.0) and 0.081 for f (0.083). Per hectare (units = :density, I₀ = 0.01 ha⁻¹) they are 0.69 and 0.006, because transmission per susceptible is then 2.1 times higher. The paper's R0 values keep S₀ per hectare, and so does basic_reproduction_number; the simulated outbreaks therefore spread as if R0 were 0.475 times the reported value. This is an inference, not printed.
  • Printed inconsistencies.
    • Table S1's s₀ = 0.9906 is not logistic(b₀) = 0.9875.
    • s₀ is said to match an 8-year life span, but that span gives 0.99897 per 3 days.
    • The simplified model's R₀ is 7.62, not 7.9.
    • "Reducing transmission and host survival by 87% each (Fig 4a)" should read 54%. The 87% is the transmission-only intercept of Fig. 4a, whose axes are transmission and density.
    • The text says the probiotic increases R0, as here (+2.6%), but Fig. 2 plots it below no action.

All of these are validation targets (canessa2018_* in data/validation/targets.toml).

The paper's ranking is reproduced:

  • only the full-coverage antifungal (d) and removal (h) give R₀ < 1 reliably;
  • 90% thinning (g) is a coin toss;
  • actions that slow Bsal growth (c–e) lengthen the distances infected adults travel (Fig. 3: 37 m to 53 m before cohort survival falls below 1%);
  • removal keeps them under 10 m.

The simplified model and Fig. 4

Collapsing the load structure gives a discrete-time SI model (eq. S11): S(t+1) = S s₀(1 − φ) and I(t+1) = S s₀ φ + I s̄, with φ = 1 − exp(−βI). Its R₀ = β(1−m_β) S₀(1−m_{S₀}) s₀(1−m_s)/(1 − s̄(1−m_s)) (eq. S12) is basic_reproduction_number(p::CanessaSIParameters; mβ, mS0, msurv). required_reduction solves eq. S12 for R₀ = 1. It reproduces Fig. 4a–c:

  • 87% for transmission or density alone;
  • 75% for survival alone;
  • 42% for all three together.

API

Chytridiomycosis.CanessaDraw — Type
CanessaDraw

One of the 1,000 archived parameter sets of Canessa et al. (2018) (Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.0jh5p, file Canessa_JAPPL_2017_00978_SI2.xls). Fields:

  • id: row number, 1-1000;
  • survival: 3-day survival probabilities at loads 0, 10, 100, 1000 and 10000 GE;
  • transmission: per-encounter transmission probabilities at the same loads;
  • Lmax: maximum load per animal (GE); growth: zoospores produced by one zoospore in 5 days;
  • β0: encounter rate (ha per 3-day step), derived from Stegen et al. (2017);
  • S0: initial density of susceptible adults (ha⁻¹), paired with β0.

The survival, transmission, Lmax and growth columns are draws from the pooled expert elicitation (beta-PERT distributions). See load_canessa2018.

Chytridiomycosis.CanessaAction — Type
CanessaAction(name; label = string(name), β_factor = 1, β_low_factor = 1,
              low_load = 100, growth_factor = 1, density_factor = 1, removal = 0)

A management action of Canessa et al. (2018, Table 1), as changes to the IPM:

  • β_factor: multiplies the transmission function β(z) at every load;
  • β_low_factor: multiplies β(z) for loads at or below low_load (GE);
  • growth_factor: multiplies the elicited zoospores-per-zoospore value before the growth function is refitted (Table 1's λ = (⁵√(0.2·data))³ is growth_factor = 0.2);
  • density_factor: multiplies the initial density S0 (removal before Bsal arrives);
  • removal: the proportion of all adults removed at each step after Bsal arrives, so that s0 and s(z) are multiplied by 1 - removal.

canessa_actions builds the eight actions of Table 1 from the compendium.

Chytridiomycosis.canessa_actions — Function
canessa_actions(key = :canessa2018) -> Vector{CanessaAction}

The eight exploratory actions of Table 1, with values from the compendium:

  • a no action;
  • b improve body condition: 0.5 β(z) for z ≤ 100 GE;
  • c probiotic: as b, and Bsal growth slowed by 80% (growth_factor = 0.2);
  • d antifungal, full coverage: (1 - 0.98) β(z) and growth slowed by 80%;
  • e antifungal, 80% coverage: (1 - 0.8 · 0.98) β(z) and growth slowed by 80%;
  • f, g pre-emptive thinning: 0.5 S0 and 0.1 S0;
  • h post-detection removal: 90% of all adults per step (0.1 s(z), and 0.1 s0).

Chytridiomycosis.CanessaParameters — Type
CanessaParameters(; b0, b1, s0 = logistic(b0), b2, b3, β0, μG0, σ2G0, b4, b5, b6, Lmax,
                  σ2G, b7, S0, β_low_factor = 1, low_load = 100, removal = 0,
                  step_days = 3)
CanessaParameters(:canessa2018; action = canessa_action(:a))
CanessaParameters(d::CanessaDraw; action = canessa_action(:a), s0 = :fit, key = :canessa2018)

The functions of the Canessa et al. (2018) integral projection model for one parameter set, on the log-load scale z = ln(load in GE). Probabilities and β0 are per step of step_days = 3 days, and densities per hectare.

  • Survival (eq. S4): s(z) = (1 - removal) logistic(b0 + b1 z); susceptibles survive with s0 (already multiplied by 1 - removal).
  • Transmission (eqs. S3, S5): β(z) = β0 logistic(b2 + b3 z), times β_low_factor for z ≤ ln(low_load); β0 is in ha per step.
  • Initial load (eq. S7): G0 = N(μG0, σ2G0).
  • Growth (eqs. S9, S10): z' ~ N(b4 + b5 z + b6 z², σ2G exp(b7 z)) for z < ln Lmax, and N(ln Lmax, σ2G exp(b7 ln Lmax)) above.
  • S0: initial density of susceptible adults (ha⁻¹).

The keyed constructor gives Table S1, the fits to the experts' best estimates (s0 = 0.9906 as printed). An action with growth_factor ≠ 1 refits b4-b6 from the best estimates Lmax = 10⁴ and 25 zoospores per zoospore, inferred from Table S1.

The CanessaDraw constructor refits every function to one archived draw, as each of the paper's 1,000 runs did: logistic regressions of the elicited survival and transmission probabilities on z (load 0 at z = -1), and eq. S9 from the draw's Lmax and growth (canessa_growth_coefficients). s0 = :fit (default) takes s(0) of the survival fit, as Table S1 describes; s0 = :elicited takes the elicited survival at load 0. The action's factors are applied on top. G0 and the variance come from the compendium.

Chytridiomycosis.canessa_growth_coefficients — Function
canessa_growth_coefficients(Lmax, growth; steps = 10, L0 = 10, step_days = 3,
                            growth_days = 5) -> (b4, b5, b6)

The expected next log-load z' = b4 + b5 z + b6 z² of eq. S9. A Beverton-Holt series L(t+1) = λ L(t)/(1 + (λ - 1) L(t)/Lmax) (eq. S8) with λ = (growth^(1/growth_days))^step_days is run for steps steps from L0, and ln L(t+1) is regressed on ln L(t) (quadratic, least squares). The paper does not give steps; 10 steps with Lmax = 10⁴ and growth = 25 reproduce Table S1 (1.0194, 1.5288, -0.0692).

Chytridiomycosis.CanessaIPMProblem — Type
CanessaIPMProblem(p::CanessaParameters; units = :transect, I0 = nothing, nsteps = 30,
                  mesh = (-10, 15, 300), cap = :truncate)

The Canessa et al. (2018) integral projection model (eq. S2) for parameters p, starting from S0 susceptible and I0 infected adults with loads distributed as G0, for nsteps steps of 3 days. The integrals use the midpoint rule on mesh = (lower, upper, n); cap is as in basic_reproduction_number.

units sets what the transmission term ∫ β(z) I(z) dz counts:

  • :transect (default): numbers of adults on the 0.475-ha transect of Stegen et al. (2017) (475 m × 10 m), from which S0 and β0 were derived. The population starts with S0 × 0.475 susceptibles and, by default, I0 = 1 infected adult. This is an inference (not printed): eq. S6 gives the printed β0 = 0.007 only when it is solved with the transect count (see final_size_transmission), and simulating in the same units reproduces the final densities of Fig. 2 (actions e and f).
  • :density: adults per hectare, as Table S1 labels S0 (503 ha⁻¹) and I0 (one per km², I0 = 0.01 ha⁻¹ by default). With β0 per hectare, every outbreak runs 1/0.475 = 2.1 times faster than with :transect.

basic_reproduction_number keeps S0 per hectare, as the paper's R0 values do; with units = :transect the outbreak therefore spreads as if R0 were 0.475 times the reported value.

solve(prob) iterates the model and returns a named tuple: t (days), S (susceptibles), I (infected), φ (infection probability in each step), z (mesh) and n, the length(z) × (nsteps + 1) matrix of infected adults in each mesh cell, all in units.

Chytridiomycosis.canessa_dispersal — Function
canessa_dispersal(p::CanessaParameters; v = 0.5, threshold = 0.01,
                  mesh = (-10, 15, 300), cap = :truncate)
    -> (; distance, proportion, lifespan, max_distance, mean_distance)

How far infected adults move before dying (Fig. 3): proportion[k] of a newly infected cohort moves at least distance[k] metres. Adults move v km yr⁻¹ (Table S1, 0.5; a 365-day year), so 3 v/365 km per step. lifespan is the number of steps until cumulative survival falls below threshold, max_distance the distance covered in that time, and mean_distance the expected distance (v times the expected steps survived).

Chytridiomycosis.required_reduction — Function
required_reduction(p::CanessaSIParameters, which; mβ = 0, mS0 = 0, msurv = 0)

The proportional reduction that brings eq. S12 to R0 = 1 (Fig. 4a-c). which names what is solved for: :β, :S0 or :survival (the others held at the given values), or a tuple of these, all reduced by the same proportion (for example (:β, :S0, :survival)). Chytridiomycosis.final_size_transmission — Function
final_size_transmission(S0, S∞, s̄) -> β

Eq. S6, the final-size relation ln(S0/S∞) = R0 (1 - S∞/S0) with R0 = S0 β/(1 - s̄) (SI line 70), solved for β: β = (1 - s̄) ln(S0/S∞)/(S0 - S∞). The paper used it with data from Stegen et al. (2017) to derive the encounter rate β0.

β is per unit of whatever S0 and S∞ count. With Table S1's S0 = 503 ha⁻¹, S∞ = 7 and s̄ = 0.542 it is 0.0039, not the printed 0.007. With S0 as the count on the 0.475-ha transect, 503 × 0.475 = 238.9 adults, and S∞ = 7, it is 0.00697. The archived draws agree with that reading (validation targets canessa2018_beta0_*).
