Several hosts, one zoospore pool (Daversa et al. 2022)

Daversa et al. (2022, Am Nat 200(4), doi:10.1086/720638) ran mesocosms of fire salamander, spiny toad and Iberian tree frog larvae, which they inoculated with Bd. They fitted a model in which every species draws on, and sheds into, one zoospore pool. For tank j (time in days, numbers per 80 L tank):

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{dH_i}{dt} &= -\mu_i H_i,\\ \frac{dI_i}{dt} &= \beta_i (H_i - I_i) Z - I_i(\mu_i + \sigma_i),\\ \frac{dZ}{dt} &= \sum_k \lambda_k I_k + \Phi(t) - Z\Big(\gamma + \sum_k H_k \beta_k\Big). \end{aligned}\]

The equations were checked against the PDF and against the authors' R code (Zenodo 5068208).

MultiHostPoolProblem takes any number of species. The inoculations Φ(t) are pulses given by doses.

Reproduction numbers

The contribution of species i is

\[R_{0,i} = \frac{\beta_i \lambda_i H_i}{(\mu_i + \sigma_i)\,(\gamma + \sum_k H_k \beta_k)}, \qquad R_{0,\mathrm{TOT}} = \sum_i R_{0,i}.\]

The paper (p. 6) prints μ_i alone in the denominator. The authors' code uses μ_i + σ_i, and so does the next-generation matrix. Only μ_i + σ_i reproduces Fig. 4b. The total is the sum of the contributions, and it is also the spectral radius: all transmission passes through the one pool, so the host-to-host next-generation matrix has rank one. next_generation_matrix computes the NGM numerically from the right-hand side, and the tests check that it equals the sum. The method of basic_reproduction_number for DaversaParameters returns that sum.

Parameters

The paper reports its estimates only as a figure (Fig. 4a), and the supplement could not be obtained. DaversaParameters(:daversa2022) therefore holds posterior medians digitised from that figure, which the compendium marks as unverified. At these values:

  • the fit reproduces the paper's r = 0.70 and R² = 0.50;
  • per-tank R0 tracks the Fig. 4c–d medians (r = 0.9996), lying about 7% below them.

See data/validation/targets.toml for the targets.

Chytridiomycosis.DaversaParameters — Type
DaversaParameters(; species, β, λ, σ, γ)
DaversaParameters(:daversa2022; set = "")

Parameters of the Daversa et al. (2022) shared-pool model for any number of host species.

  • species: the species names.
  • β: transmission rates, in zoospore⁻¹ day⁻¹. This is the infection rate per susceptible host per zoospore in the tank, and also each host's rate of removing zoospores.
  • λ: shedding rates, in zoospores per infected host per day.
  • σ: recovery rates, in day⁻¹. Recovered hosts become susceptible again.
  • γ: zoospore mortality, in day⁻¹.

The keyed constructor reads the three species (salamander, toad, frog) and γ from the compendium. Its β, λ and σ are posterior medians digitised from Fig. 4a: the paper prints no numbers, so treat them as approximate. Host loss rates μ vary from tank to tank and belong to the problem, not to these parameters.

Chytridiomycosis.MultiHostPoolProblem — Type
MultiHostPoolProblem(p::DaversaParameters, H0; I0 = zeros, Z0 = 0, μ = zeros,
                     tspan = (0.0, 30.0), doses = Tuple{Float64,Float64}[])

The Daversa et al. (2022) model for one tank: n = length(p.species) host species sharing one zoospore pool.

  • H0: initial numbers of hosts.
  • I0: initial numbers infected.
  • Z0: initial zoospores.
  • μ: per-species host loss rates, in day⁻¹ (death plus metamorphosis). The paper estimates them per tank as ln(H(0)/H(T))/T.
  • doses: (day, zoospores) pulses, which make up the inoculation term Φ(t).

The state is [H₁…Hₙ, I₁…Iₙ, Z]. solve(prob) returns the ODESolution, and to_ode_problem(prob) the ODEProblem with the dosing callback attached. Use daversa_doses for the inoculation schedule of the published fit.

daversa_doses(key = :daversa2022) -> (; Z0, doses)

The inoculations used in the authors' fit, from the compendium: Z0 zoospores at day 0, then doses as (day, zoospores) pulses on days 10, 17 and 25.

These come from the authors' R code. The paper text instead says three inoculations, on days 1, 11 and 18, of 18,000 to 24,000 zoospores L⁻¹ in 80 L.

Chytridiomycosis.species_reproduction_numbers — Function
species_reproduction_numbers(p::DaversaParameters, H; μ = zeros, as_printed = false)

The contribution of each species to Bd's basic reproduction number in a tank with host numbers H and loss rates μ (day⁻¹):

R0_i = β_i λ_i H_i / ((μ_i + σ_i) (γ + Σ_k H_k β_k))

This is the formula in the authors' code. It is also what the next-generation matrix of the model gives (see next_generation_matrix). Each term multiplies three things: the infections a zoospore causes in species i during its lifetime, the time an infected host stays infected, and its shedding.

The formula printed on p. 6 has μ_i alone in the denominator, which drops recovery. Pass as_printed = true to use it. It overstates R0 about twentyfold when σ ≈ 0.7 day⁻¹.

The total R0_TOT is the sum of the contributions (basic_reproduction_number).

Chytridiomycosis.next_generation_matrix — Function
next_generation_matrix(p::DaversaParameters, H; μ = zeros, spores = :transition)
    -> (; K, R0)

The next-generation matrix K = F V⁻¹ of the model at the disease-free state, computed numerically from the right-hand side. It does not use the R0 formula. The infected compartments are (I₁…Iₙ, Z), and host numbers are held at H. The Jacobian of the right-hand side is taken by central differences, which are exact here because the model is bilinear. F is the Jacobian of the new-infection terms alone, and V = F − J.

  • spores = :transition: only host infections are new; shedding moves an infection from a host into the pool. Then R0 = ρ(K) equals Σ_i R0_i.
  • spores = :generation: shedding also counts as a new generation (host → spore → host). Then R0 = ρ(K) = √(Σ_i R0_i). Both give the same threshold at 1.

Chytridiomycosis.tank_reproduction_numbers — Function
tank_reproduction_numbers(p::DaversaParameters,
                          tanks = load_daversa2022(; three_species_only = true);
                          μ = :code, as_printed = false) -> Vector{Vector{Float64}}

The species contributions R0_i (species_reproduction_numbers) for each tank, at the tank's initial host numbers. The loss rates are set by μ:

  • μ = :code: as in the authors' R0calc. Salamanders use the tank's own loss rate. Toads and frogs use the mean loss rate over all 36 tanks, which counts as 0 the tanks where the species is absent.
  • μ = :tank: each species uses its own loss rate in the tank.

The mesocosm data

The Dryad deposit (10.5061/dryad.2v6wwpznk, CC0) ships in data/daversa2022/. It is prepared as in the authors' code.

Chytridiomycosis.MesocosmTank — Type
MesocosmTank

One tank of the Daversa et al. (2022) experiment, prepared as in Part 4 of the authors' R code. Fields:

  • tank and treatment.
  • H0 and Hfinal: the numbers of salamanders, toads and frogs at the start and at the end.
  • day_final: the final sampling day, counted from 2017-07-04 (day 0).
  • prev_initial and prev_final: the prevalence among sampled animals (bd.load > 0). It is NaN where a species was not sampled; toads and frogs were sampled only at the end.
  • μ: loss rates in day⁻¹, ln(H0/Hfinal)/day_final, and 0 where a species is absent.

Chytridiomycosis.load_daversa2022 — Function
load_daversa2022(; three_species_only = false) -> Vector{MesocosmTank}

Read the 36 tanks, or with three_species_only = true the 21 three-species tanks used in the fit. The data preparation follows Part 4 of the authors' R code:

  • drop the accidental samples;
  • count day 0 from the first sampling date;
  • call an animal infected when its load is above 0;
  • take each tank's final day as the earliest final sampling date in that tank;
  • compute exponential loss rates from the initial and final numbers.

Chytridiomycosis.daversa_loglikelihood — Function
daversa_loglikelihood(p::DaversaParameters, tanks) -> Float64

The authors' log-likelihood (R code, tankLL): a binomial over the final numbers infected, summed over species and tanks. It is written exactly as in the code:

  • the number of trials is the initial abundance H0, not the final one;
  • the success probability is I(T)/H0;
  • 1 − p is floored at 0.01;
  • the observed counts may be fractional, because they are prevalence × final abundance.

The binomial coefficient is omitted.
