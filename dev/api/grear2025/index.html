Hurdle model of Bd across species (Grear 2025)

Grear et al. (2025, Ecological Monographs 95:e70001, doi:10.1002/ecm.70001) swabbed 11,183 amphibians of 54 species across the United States. They fitted a Bayesian hurdle-lognormal model with brms to the Bd quantity per swab. It has two parts:

  • a logistic part for whether a swab is positive (prevalence);
  • a lognormal part for the load of positive swabs (intensity).

Both parts use the same covariates:

  • age and sex;
  • the mean daily maximum temperature over the 30 days before sampling, and its square;
  • precipitation over the previous 10 days;
  • random intercepts for site, NOAA region, and species (phylogenetically correlated).

The model is checked against the published full text, and the authors' USGS code release (doi:10.5066/P1MXUJZM, CC0) supplies the data file, which is bundled in data/grear2025. Their formula and priors agree with eqs 1–10 of the paper. Two differences between paper and code are switches:

  • The σ prior (sigma_prior). The paper puts the half-Student-t(3, 0, 2.5) prior on the variance σ² (:as_printed). The code leaves brms' default, which is on σ (:as_coded, the default).
  • The phylogeny (tree in grear2025_phylogeny). The paper used 30 trees, one per imputed data set. The code's file has 100 trees, and its loop over them is broken.

Missing sex, life stage and genus-only Taricha were multiply imputed in the paper (30 data sets). Grear2025Problem draws one such data set by the authors' rule (grear2025_impute); missing_covariates = :drop drops those swabs instead.

What is checked:

  • Counts. Swabs (11,183), species (54), sites (593, in the Results) and genus-only records (42) are reproduced.
    • The abstract's 609 sites contradicts the Results and is not found anywhere in the data or code.
    • The modelled sample (11,172 against 11,175) and the missing sex and life-stage counts (191 and 123 against 159 and 91) are discrepant.
  • Temperature. Both parts are concave in temperature, and prevalence peaks at 14.6 °C, against "approximately 15 °C" in the paper.
  • Pending. The printed 95% credible intervals of the 14 fixed effects, the intensity optimum (13 °C) and the PSIS-LOO table need the full posterior (scripts/grear2025_nuts.jl). Those targets carry the printed values.
The posterior mode does not converge

Many sites, and some species, have only negative swabs. Their hurdle random effects and the random-effect standard deviations keep growing, so posterior_mode does not reach a finite mode. After 3000 iterations the site standard deviation of the prevalence part exceeds 70 on the logit scale.

The prevalence fixed effects settle within a few hundred iterations. The intensity ones drift: the intensity optimum is 11.9, 14.0 and 15.9 °C after 500, 1000 and 3000 iterations. For that reason only prevalence quantities are compared numerically, and the printed credible intervals are compared only with a full posterior.

Hurdle regression layer

HurdleRegressionProblem adds covariates and random intercepts to the two-part model of LoadHurdleModel, in brms' hurdle_lognormal parametrisation. There, hu is the probability of a zero, so a prevalence coefficient is minus the corresponding hu coefficient.

The problem is a LogDensityProblems target with an analytic gradient, so any gradient-based sampler can draw from the full posterior. posterior_mode finds its mode with L-BFGS.

Chytridiomycosis.HurdleRegressionProblem — Type
HurdleRegressionProblem(y, X, groups; names = ["x1", …], prior_b = 20, prior_a = 20,
                        prior_b_hu = 1, prior_a_hu = 1, prior_scale = 2.5,
                        prior_scale_hu = 2.5, sigma_prior = :sd)

Hurdle-lognormal regression of swab loads y (0 = not detected) on the columns of X (no intercept column; the same design for both parts), in the parametrisation of brms' hurdle_lognormal (hu, the probability of a zero, is logit-linear; positive loads are lognormal with log-mean linear and standard deviation σ). groups is a vector of random intercepts, each a NamedTuple (name, index, levels, correlation): index[i] is swab i's level, levels the number of levels and correlation nothing (independent levels) or a levels×levels correlation matrix (e.g. a phylogenetic one). Each group has its own standard deviation in each part.

Priors (all on the scale of the linear predictors): b ~ N(0, prior_b²) and a ~ N(0, prior_a²) for the load part; b_hu ~ N(0, prior_b_hu²), a_hu ~ N(0, prior_a_hu²) for the hurdle part; half-Student-t(3, 0, prior_scale) on σ and the load-part standard deviations and half-Student-t(3, 0, prior_scale_hu) on the hurdle-part ones. With sigma_prior = :variance the half-Student-t prior on σ is put on σ² instead.

It is a LogDensityProblems target with gradient (LogDensityOrder{1}) on the unconstrained parameters prob.names (standard deviations on the log scale, with their Jacobian). Use posterior_mode for a MAP fit, hurdle_coefficients for the fixed effects on the uncentred scale.

Chytridiomycosis.posterior_mode — Method
posterior_mode(prob; x0 = nothing, iters = 5000, gtol = 1e-5, memory = 10) -> NamedTuple

Maximum a posteriori estimate of a LogDensityProblems target with gradient (such as a HurdleRegressionProblem) by L-BFGS with backtracking. Returns x, logdensity, gradient and iterations. For hierarchical models the mode is on the unconstrained (log-sd, non-centred) scale, not a summary of the posterior.

Chytridiomycosis.grear2025_impute — Function
grear2025_impute(data; rng = Random.Xoshiro(2025)) -> Grear2025Data

One imputed copy of the swab data, as in the authors' 08_ImputeData.R (Methods, "Imputation for missing data"): a swab missing Age and/or Sex gets a draw from the observed frequencies of the Age×Sex combinations of its genus (keeping the recorded one of the two when only one is missing); in a genus with no complete record (Spea) Age is Adult, Juvenile or Larval with equal probability and Sex Unknown. Chytridiomycosis.Grear2025Problem — Type
Grear2025Problem(data = load_grear2025(); random = (:species, :site, :region),
                 phylogeny = nothing, missing_covariates = :impute,
                 rng = Random.Xoshiro(2025), sigma_prior = :as_coded)

The hurdle-lognormal model of Grear et al. (2025, eqs 1–10) as a HurdleRegressionProblem (log density with gradient, LogDensityProblems interface). Both parts have the fixed effects Age (Juvenile, Larval; reference Adult), Sex (Male, Unknown; reference Female), 30-day mean maximum temperature and its square, and 10-day precipitation, with the printed priors (normal(0, 20) load part, normal(0, 1) prevalence part; compendium grear2025). random picks the random intercepts: :species (correlated by phylogeny, a (; species, correlation) from grear2025_phylogeny, or independent when nothing), :site and :region.

missing_covariates = :impute (default) fills missing Age/Sex and "Taricha sp." with one draw of grear2025_impute using rng; :drop drops those swabs. Swabs of a species absent from phylogeny are dropped. sigma_prior = :as_coded puts the half-Student-t(3, 0, 2.5) prior on σ (brms' default, as in the authors' code); :as_printed puts it on σ² as the paper states. The swab data used are in prob.data.
