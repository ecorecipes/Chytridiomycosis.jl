Seasonal Bsal and a Floquet R0 (Bozzuto & Canessa 2019)

Bozzuto & Canessa (2019, Glob Ecol Conserv 17:e00551, doi:10.1016/j.gecco.2019.e00551) asked how seasonal cycles change the invasion threshold of B. salamandrivorans in fire salamanders, and how much management is needed to keep a population free of it. Time t is in decimal years from the start of January, and densities are per hectare:

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{dS}{dt} &= v(t) - dS - c(t)p(t)SI,\\ \frac{dI}{dt} &= c(t)p(t)SI - (\delta(t) + d)I. \end{aligned}\]

Each seasonal coefficient has the form θ(t) = θ₀(1 − θ₁ cos 2π(t − ℓθ)). The proportion of contacts that transmit is p(t) = p₀(1 − p₁ cos 2πt) (the paper sets p₁ = 0). Bsal-induced mortality is δ(t) = δ₀(1 − δ₁ cos 2πt), and natural mortality d is constant. The contact rate has two peaks, in spring and in autumn:

\[c(t) = \frac{c_0}{m'}\,(1 - c_1\cos 2\pi t)\,(1 - c_2\cos 2\pi(t - \ell_c)).\]

Larval inflow v(t) is a step, v between t₁ and t₂ (late summer). The equations and values were checked against the PDF and the online Appendix A.

SeasonalBsalProblem lowers the model to an ODEProblem. seasonal = false gives the non-seasonal model.

Two things the PDF does not make obvious

  • The contact rate is rescaled. Table 1 prints c(t) without the factor 1/m'. Appendix A1 divides by m', half the sum of the minimum and maximum of c(t)/c₀, so that c₀ lies midway between the lowest and highest contact rate. Eq. (2) holds only for the rescaled form. scaled_contact = false gives the Table 1 formula as printed.
  • Table 1 prints the wrong c₂ for the hibernating population. It prints 0.90. The Fig. A4 caption gives 0.95, and only 0.95 reproduces the Floquet-R0 of 8.27, the numerical 8.33 and the elasticities of Fig. A5. The package uses 0.95. as_printed = true uses 0.90, which gives 8.67.

Floquet-R0

The infected equation, linearised at the disease-free state, is dI/dt = J(t) I with J(t) = c(t)p(t)S(t) − d − δ(t). Its Floquet exponent ξ is the growth of log I over one year, and Bsal cannot invade when ξ < 0. Rearranging ξ < 0 as a ratio gives the paper's Floquet-R0, and with S = k₀ and p₁ = 0 it is eq. (2):

\[\text{Floquet-}R_0 = R_0\,\frac{2 + c_1c_2\cos 2\pi\ell_c}{m}, \qquad R_0 = \frac{c_0p_0k_0}{d + \delta_0},\qquad m = 2m'.\]

Here R₀ is the paper's non-seasonal reproduction number. The infected subsystem has only one compartment, so the Floquet exponent is simply a time average. Three consequences follow:

  • The paper's numerical Floquet-R0, ∫ c p S dt / ∫ (d + δ) dt, is exactly the periodic R0 of Bacaër and Guernaoui (the spectral radius of the next-generation operator). method = :spectral computes it the general way, from Floquet multipliers, and agrees to within 1e-7.
  • The analytic Floquet-R0 equals the R0 of the model with every coefficient replaced by its annual mean (averaged_reproduction_number). The roughly 7% gap between Floquet-R0 and the paper's R₀ exists because c₀ is the midrange of the contact rate, not its mean. Seasonality as such, the covariance of contacts with host density, adds less than 1% (8.33 against 8.26 for the hibernating population).
  • δ₁ has no effect on the result, as the paper notes.

Every aestivation value in the package is about 1.6% below the paper's, while the seasonal ratios agree (Floquet-R0/R0 is 0.901 in both). The paper's numbers all imply R0 ≈ 2.127. Three single changes would give that:

  • k₀ = 253.8. Ruled out: the numerical Floquet-R0 would stay at 1.89, because S(t) comes from the printed inflow v = 184.
  • c₀ = 0.3703. Reproduces every printed number.
  • A winter infective period of 28.7 days instead of 26 (δ₀ = 42.74, δ₁ = 0.703). Also reproduces every printed number.

The R0(t) curve of Fig. 2b, digitised in data/bozzuto2019, separates the last two. The longer infective period matches its shape to about 2 pixels (RMS 0.022). A larger c₀ leaves the curve 0.2–0.3 too low in winter (RMS 0.12). Fitting both infective periods to the curve gives 28.7 and 4.95 days. The same fit on Fig. 2a gives 39.8 and 4.95 days, against the documented 40 and 5.

No documented calculation gives 28.7 days. Appendix A1's interpolation gives 26 days. The package keeps 26, and these targets stay discrepant in data/validation/targets.toml.

Chytridiomycosis.BozzutoParameters — Type
BozzutoParameters(; c0, c1, c2, ℓc, p0 = 1, p1 = 0, k0, v, t1, t2, d, δ0, δ1)
BozzutoParameters(:bozzuto2019; population = :hibernation, activity = :table1,
                  as_printed = false)

Parameters of the Bozzuto & Canessa (2019) seasonal Bsal model (eq. 1, Table 1). Time is in decimal years and densities are per hectare.

  • c0 (ha yr⁻¹), c1, c2, ℓc (yr): the contact rate c(t) = (c0/m') (1 - c1 cos 2πt)(1 - c2 cos 2π(t - ℓc)). c1 sets the winter trough, c2 the trough lagged by ℓc, and m' (contact_scaling) makes c0 the midrange.
  • p0, p1: the proportion of contacts that transmit, p(t) = p0 (1 - p1 cos 2πt).
  • k0 (ha⁻¹): average carrying capacity, used where the disease-free density is held fixed.
  • v (larvae ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹), t1, t2 (yr): the step larval inflow, v on [t1, t2].
  • d (yr⁻¹): natural mortality, constant.
  • δ0 (yr⁻¹), δ1: Bsal-induced mortality δ(t) = δ0 (1 - δ1 cos 2πt).

The keyed constructor reads the compendium. population is :hibernation (north-western Europe) or :aestivation (coastal NE Spain). activity = :high substitutes the c1, c2 of Fig. A4 (~25% activity in winter and summer). as_printed = true uses Table 1's c2 = 0.90 for the hibernating population instead of the 0.95 that reproduces the paper's results (see the compendium notes).

Chytridiomycosis.SeasonalBsalProblem — Type
SeasonalBsalProblem(p::BozzutoParameters; I0 = 0.01, u0 = nothing, tspan = (0.0, 1.0),
                    inflow = :step, scaled_contact = true, seasonal = true)

The Bozzuto & Canessa (2019) model, eq. (1), with state [S, I] in salamanders per hectare and time in years from the start of January.

  • u0 defaults to the disease-free S(t0) of inflow (disease_free_susceptibles) plus I0 infected. The paper simulates no outbreak, so the default I0 = 0.01 ha⁻¹ (one per km²) is only a convenient small introduction.
  • inflow: :step, :sinusoidal or :constant (larval_inflow).
  • scaled_contact = false uses Table 1's contact formula without the 1/m' of Appendix A1.
  • seasonal = false gives the non-seasonal model: c0, p0, δ0 and inflow d k0.

solve(prob) returns the ODESolution. to_ode_problem(prob) gives the ODEProblem; the times where a step inflow switches on and off are passed as tstops.

Chytridiomycosis.floquet_exponent — Function
floquet_exponent(p::BozzutoParameters; method = :analytic, inflow = ...)

The Floquet exponent ξ of the infected equation linearised at the disease-free state: log y(1) for dy/dt = (c(t) p(t) S(t) - d - δ(t)) y, y(0) = 1 (eq. A3; the period is one year). Bsal cannot invade when ξ < 0.

  • method = :analytic: eq. (A4a), ξ = (c0 p0 k0/2m')(2 - c1 k1 + c1 p1 - k1 p1 + cos 2πℓc (c1 c2 - c2 k1 + c2 p1 - 3c1c2k1p1/4)) - δ0 - d, with k1 = 0 for inflow = :constant (the paper's choice, eq. A4c) and k1 = d/2π for inflow = :sinusoidal (eq. A2b).
  • method = :numerical: integrates eq. (A3) for the disease-free S(t) of inflow (default :step, eq. A1).

Chytridiomycosis.floquet_reproduction_number — Function
floquet_reproduction_number(p::BozzutoParameters; method = :analytic, inflow = ...)

The paper's Floquet-R0: the rearrangement of the Floquet exponent that is below one exactly when ξ < 0, Floquet-R0 = 1 + ξ/(d + δ0).

  • method = :analytic (default inflow = :constant): eq. (2), R0 (2 + c1 c2 cos 2πℓc)/m when p1 = 0.
  • method = :numerical (default inflow = :step): ∫ c p S dt / ∫ (d + δ) dt over a year, with S(t) from eq. (A1), as in the paper's numerical check.
  • method = :spectral (default inflow = :step): the Bacaër–Guernaoui periodic R0, the R for which the infected subsystem with transmission divided by R has dominant Floquet multiplier one, from periodic_reproduction_number. For this scalar subsystem it equals :numerical; it is here as an independent check.

Chytridiomycosis.averaged_reproduction_number — Function
averaged_reproduction_number(p::BozzutoParameters; inflow = :constant)

The R0 of the model with every coefficient replaced by its annual mean: mean(c) mean(p) mean(S)/(d + δ0). With S = k0 and p1 = 0 this equals the analytical Floquet-R0 of eq. (2): the infected subsystem is scalar, so its Floquet exponent is a time average. With inflow = :step it differs from the numerical Floquet-R0 only by the covariance of c(t) with S(t).

seasonal_factor(c1, c2, ℓc)
seasonal_factor(p::BozzutoParameters)

The ratio Floquet-R0/R0 of eq. (2), (2 + c1 c2 cos 2πℓc)/m with m = 2m' (contact_scaling). It is the mean of the rescaled contact cycle c(t)/c0. The paper finds that it lies between about 2/3 and 4/3 (±33%).

Chytridiomycosis.target_proportion — Function
target_proportion(p::BozzutoParameters; basis = :floquet, kwargs...)

The proportion of the susceptible density to keep (by removal, say) so that Bsal cannot invade, capped at one (§2.3):

  • basis = :floquet: ϱF = 1/Floquet-R0 (kwargs go to floquet_reproduction_number);
  • basis = :nonseasonal: ϱ = 1/R0;
  • basis = :peak: 1/max_t R0(t), which also prevents temporary outbreaks (kwargs go to instantaneous_reproduction_number).

Chytridiomycosis.contact_scaling — Function
contact_scaling(p::BozzutoParameters)
contact_scaling(c1, c2, ℓc)

The factor m' = (min c(t)/c0 + max c(t)/c0)/2 of the unscaled two-peaked contact rate c0 (1 - c1 cos 2πt)(1 - c2 cos 2π(t - ℓc)). Appendix A1 divides the contact rate by m' so that c0 lies midway between its minimum and maximum. The paper found no closed form and computed it numerically, as here (a grid refined by golden-section search). The m of eq. (2) is 2m'.

Chytridiomycosis.larval_inflow — Function
larval_inflow(p::BozzutoParameters, t; inflow = :step)

The inflow of metamorphs, in larvae ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹.

  • :step: Table 1's step function, v while t1 ≤ t mod 1 ≤ t2 and zero otherwise. The paper uses it in its numerical analysis.
  • :sinusoidal: Appendix A2's approximation v0 (1 - cos 2π(t - 1/4)), peaking in September. The paper does not give v0; here v0 = d k0, the value that makes the mean disease-free density k0.
  • :constant: d k0, the non-seasonal model.

Chytridiomycosis.disease_free_susceptibles — Function
disease_free_susceptibles(p::BozzutoParameters, t; inflow = :step)

The periodic disease-free density S(t) (ha⁻¹), the solution of dS/dt = v(t) - d S with S(0) = S(1).

  • :step: eq. (A1), with S(0) = A/(1 - e^{-d}) and A = (v/d)(e^{-d(1-t2)} - e^{-d(1-t1)}). Its mean is v (t2 - t1)/d, which is 499.2 for the hibernating population, against k0 = 500.
  • :sinusoidal: the exact periodic solution for the sinusoidal inflow, k0 + k0 d (2π cos 2πt - d sin 2πt)/(d² + 4π²). Eq. (A2b), k0 (1 + (d/2π) cos 2πt), is this with d² = 0.
  • :constant: k0. This is what eq. (2) assumes.

Floquet tools for any periodic model

These functions take matrix-valued functions of time. Appendix A4 of the paper uses the same construction for a model with an environmental zoospore reservoir.

Chytridiomycosis.floquet_multipliers — Function
floquet_multipliers(A, n; period = 1.0, tstops = Float64[], abstol = 1e-10,
                    reltol = 1e-10)

The Floquet multipliers of the linear periodic system dx/dt = A(t) x in n dimensions: the eigenvalues of the monodromy matrix Φ(period), where dΦ/dt = A(t) Φ, Φ(0) = I (eqs. A6a–c). A(t) returns an n × n matrix. The disease-free state is stable when every multiplier has modulus below one. Pass the times where A jumps as tstops.

source
Chytridiomycosis.periodic_reproduction_number — Function
periodic_reproduction_number(F, V, n; period = 1.0, tstops = Float64[],
                             rtol = 1e-10, kwargs...)

The basic reproduction number of a periodic model (Bacaër & Guernaoui 2006; Wang & Zhao 2008): the R at which dx/dt = (F(t)/R - V(t)) x has dominant Floquet multiplier one. F(t) is the n × n matrix of new infections and V(t) of transitions out of the infected compartments, at the disease-free state. Found by bisection on log R. kwargs go to floquet_multipliers.
