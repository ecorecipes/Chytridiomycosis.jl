Published models

Reference implementations of published models, checked against the papers. Parameters are read from the compendium, for example SchmidtParameters(:schmidt2017).

Bsal in fire salamanders (Schmidt et al. 2017)

Two Results values are not reproduced (see data/validation/targets.toml):

  • S_eq. The paper gives 1.0027. Eq. (2.3) gives 1.0030. The paper's value equals (d + e)(d + d_I)/(βe), the large-K limit of eq. (2.3), so the authors probably reported that approximation.
  • K_H. The paper gives about 9.5. The Jacobian gives 10.21. No rate, unit or model variant tested gives 9.5. The equilibrium is barely stable below 10.21: the real part of the eigenvalues is −0.0011 yr⁻¹ at K = 9.5.
Chytridiomycosis.SchmidtParameters — Type
SchmidtParameters(; b, d, d_I, e, β, K, D = 0.0625, μ = 1.0)
SchmidtParameters(:schmidt2017)

Parameters of the Schmidt et al. (2017) model (eq. 2.1): recruitment b, natural death d, Bsal death d_I, latent→infectious rate e (all yr⁻¹), mass-action transmission β (ha yr⁻¹), carrying capacity K (females ha⁻¹), diffusion D (km² yr⁻¹) and scattered-colony rate μ (yr⁻¹). The density-dependence strength is γ = (b - d)/K.

source
basic_reproduction_number(p::SchmidtParameters, s::BsalLarvalStage)

R₀ of Bsal with the larval stage: the largest eigenvalue of the next-generation matrix for the infected classes (infected larvae, latent adults, infectious adults) at the disease-free state. Carry-over through metamorphosis counts as a transition, not a new infection. With no larval transmission this is Schmidt's K / K_threshold.

source
basic_reproduction_number(p::DaversaParameters, H; μ = zeros, as_printed = false)

R0_TOT = Σ_i R0_i, from species_reproduction_numbers. Daversa et al. (2022) define the total as this sum. Every transmission passes through the one pool, so the host-to-host next-generation matrix has rank one. Its spectral radius is therefore its trace, which is the same sum.

source
basic_reproduction_number(p::BozzutoParameters)

The non-seasonal R0 = c0 p0 k0/(d + δ0) of Bozzuto & Canessa (2019, p. 3). Because c0 is the midrange of the contact rate, not its mean, this is not the R0 of the time-averaged model; see averaged_reproduction_number.

source
basic_reproduction_number(tf::TransmissionFunction, N; removal)

R₀ of an SIR-type model with transmission tf in a susceptible population of size N, when infectious hosts leave at total rate removal (death plus recovery, same time unit as tf): transmission_coefficient(tf, N) / removal. This is the formula of Tompros et al. (2022, section 2.7), R₀ = α_{S,N} / (μ + σ) with S = N.

source
basic_reproduction_number(p::TomprosParameters)

R₀ of Tompros et al. (2022): β / (μ + σ) (frequency-dependent transmission, so it does not depend on population size). The paper reports 4.9.

source
basic_reproduction_number(p::ChowdhuryParameters; kwargs...)

R0 = max(R_j, R_a), the spectral radius of the next-generation matrix of Chowdhury et al. (2025); see stage_reproduction_numbers.

source
basic_reproduction_number(d::DiscretizedIPM, transmission) -> Float64

R0 of a discretised host–parasite IPM by the next-generation method for matrix models (Klepac & Caswell 2011, eq. 29; Wilber et al. 2016, Appendix S1): the spectral radius of F (I − P)⁻¹, where P is the infected kernel and F[i, j] = s0 G0_i t_j, with t_j (transmission, a scalar or a vector over bins) the expected number of hosts infected per time step by one infected host in bin j in a fully susceptible population. Because F has rank one, R0 = tᵀ (I − P)⁻¹ s0 G0.

source
basic_reproduction_number(p::WilberParameters; temperature, β = 9.82e-4, S = 100,
                          mesh = IPMMesh(:wilber2016), method = :full) -> Float64

R0 of the Wilber et al. (2016) IPM with density-dependent transmission between frogs, as in the authors' Appendix S1 code (R0_calculation.R): each infected frog infects β S of S susceptible frogs per step. β and S default to the compendium values of that code (β_R0, S_R0).

  • method = :full: the next-generation R0 of the discretised IPM (the method of basic_reproduction_number for a DiscretizedIPM).
  • method = :collapsed: the SIS approximation R0 = β s0 S / (1 − s̄(1 − l̄)), with s̄ and l̄ the survival and loss probabilities averaged over the stable load distribution of the IPM run with φ = 1 − exp(−β).

The authors' code uses a 50-bin mesh (IPMMesh(:wilber2016; n = 50)).

source
basic_reproduction_number(p::CanessaParameters; mesh = (-10, 15, 300), cap = :truncate)

The basic reproduction number of the IPM, R0 = S0 s0 ∫ β(z) [(I - P)⁻¹ G0](z) dz: the infections caused over its life by an adult that enters the infected class with a load from G0, in a population of S0 susceptibles (Wilber et al. 2016, Appendix S1; the next- generation operator has rank one). P is the survival-growth kernel s(z) G(z', z), discretised by the midpoint rule on mesh = (lower, upper, n) (the paper's -10, 15 and 300).

cap sets how the maximum load Lmax acts:

  • :truncate (default): no load exceeds ln Lmax. The growth kernel G(·, z) is cut at the last mesh point below ln Lmax and rescaled to its mass on the mesh, so survivors at the cap stay there. The SI does not print this, but it takes literally "the hypothesis of a maximum possible load per animal", and it reproduces Fig. 2 (median R0 9.4 against 9.6, thinning 50% 4.7 against 4.8, thinning 90% 0.94 against 0.93).
  • :printed: the formula of SI lines 116-118 alone. Above ln Lmax the expected next load is ln Lmax, but the normal kernel lets loads exceed it, where survival is lower. The median R0 is then 8.2, uniformly about 14% below the paper's.
source
basic_reproduction_number(p::CanessaSIParameters; mβ = 0, mS0 = 0, msurv = 0)

Eq. S12: R0 = β(1 - mβ) S0(1 - mS0) s0(1 - msurv)/(1 - s̄(1 - msurv)), the reproduction number of the simplified model after management reduces transmission by mβ, the initial density by mS0 and the survival of all adults (s0 and s̄) by msurv. With no management it is eq. S11's S0 s0 β/(1 - s̄); SI line 70 prints the same quantity without s0.

Chytridiomycosis.endemic_equilibrium — Function
endemic_equilibrium(p) -> (S, L, I)

The non-trivial equilibrium of eq. (2.1), eq. (2.3) of the paper: I = (b - d)(Kβe - (b + e)(b + d_I)) / (β(Kβe - b(b - d))), L = (b + d_I - βI) I / e, S = (b + e - βI)(b + d_I - βI)/(βe). (The I² terms cancel in S + L + I = K_threshold - bI/e.) Returns nothing if K ≤ K_threshold.

source
Chytridiomycosis.hopf_threshold — Function
hopf_threshold(p; Kmax = 1000p.K) -> K_H

Carrying capacity at which the endemic equilibrium loses stability (Hopf bifurcation, with K as the bifurcation parameter), found by bisection. The paper reports K_H ≈ 9.5.

source
Chytridiomycosis.wave_speed — Function
wave_speed(p) -> c (km yr⁻¹)

Speed of the travelling wave of the reaction–diffusion model with equal diffusion D for all classes (eq. 2.4): c = 2√(Dλ), where λ = (√((e - d_I)² + 4eβK) - 2b - d_I - e)/2 is the growth rate of the linearised infected subsystem at S = K.

source
Chytridiomycosis.scattered_colony_area — Function
scattered_colony_area(p, t; c = wave_speed(p), μ = p.μ) -> km²

Eq. (2.5) as printed: total infected area A(t) = (2πc²/μ)((e^{μt} - 1)/μ - t). Note: the paper's reported proportions of German habitat (≈9% at μ = 1, ≈0.1% at μ = 0.1, after 8 years, of 258 750 km²) equal this value divided by 100; see the wiki page.

population_crash(p; tspan = (0, 2), dt = 1e-4) -> (Smin, tmin)

Minimum susceptible density and when it is reached (the paper's figure 1 summary).

Spatial spread and long-distance jumps

The reaction–diffusion version (§2.2) is discretised by finite volumes and lowered to an ODEProblem; its front speed converges to wave_speed. The scattered-colony model behind eq. (2.5) is simulated as a jump process with colonies placed in a square habitat, so that overlaps can be counted.

Chytridiomycosis.SchmidtPDEProblem — Type
SchmidtPDEProblem(p; L = 8.0, n = 2000, geometry = :planar, tspan = (0.0, 0.5),
                  I0 = parameter_set(:schmidt2017).I0, x0 = L/n, u0 = nothing)

The spatio-temporal Schmidt et al. (2017) model (§2.2): eq. (2.1) with diffusion p.D (km² yr⁻¹) on all three classes, on [0, L] km cut into n finite volumes with zero-flux boundaries. geometry = :planar is the paper's one-dimensional domain (the infection starts at the reflecting boundary x = 0, i.e. the middle of a symmetric domain); geometry = :radial is concentric spread in the plane, x being the distance from the point of introduction.

The default start is the paper's: hosts at K everywhere and I0 infectious females ha⁻¹ (one per km², compendium record I0) within x0 km of the origin. Pass u0 (an n × 3 matrix with columns S, L, I) to override.

The front is very sharp at the published values (its leading edge decays as e^{-qx} with q = c/2D ≈ 89 km⁻¹), so the grid spacing L/n should be a few metres; see front_speed for checking the speed against wave_speed. solve returns the ODESolution (states are n × 3 matrices); to_ode_problem gives the ODEProblem.

front_position(prob::SchmidtPDEProblem, u; level = 0.5) -> km

Position of the invasion front in state u (an n × 3 matrix): the outermost point where susceptibles fall below level · K, linearly interpolated between cell centres. Returns 0 if nowhere has crashed yet.

Chytridiomycosis.front_speed — Function
front_speed(prob::SchmidtPDEProblem, sol; window = (tspan midpoint, tspan end),

Least-squares slope of front_position against time over the saved times of sol inside window; compare with the analytic travelling-wave speed wave_speed (eq. 2.4). Solve with dense saveat inside the window.

Chytridiomycosis.SchmidtColonyProblem — Type
SchmidtColonyProblem(p; μ = p.μ, c = wave_speed(p), tspan = (0.0, 8.0),
                     area = parameter_set(:schmidt2017).habitat_area,
                     origin = :centre, max_colonies = 10^6)

The scattered-colony model behind eq. (2.5) of Schmidt et al. (2017), as an explicit jump process. At time 0 one infected colony exists; every colony spreads locally as a disc whose radius grows at c km yr⁻¹ (eq. 2.4), and each colony independently sends infected salamanders that found a new colony at rate μ yr⁻¹. New colonies land uniformly at random in a square habitat of area km² (default: the paper's 258 750 km² of fire-salamander habitat in Germany). origin is :centre, :random or an (x, y) position in km.

The expected number of colonies is e^{μt} and the expected summed disc area is exactly eq. (2.5); the simulated union of discs inside the habitat is the area actually infected, which eq. (2.5) overestimates once colonies overlap or reach the habitat edge.

solve(prob; rng, saveat, resolution = 1.0) (saveat defaults to nine equally spaced times over tspan, i.e. years 0, 1, …, 8) returns a NamedTuple with the founding times and positions (km) of all colonies, and at each time in saveat: colonies, summed_area (km², the no-overlap area of eq. 2.5), infected_area (km², the union of discs clipped to the habitat, rasterised at resolution km; NaN when resolution = nothing) and fraction = infected_area / area.

Pool volume V; zoospore–frog encounter rate γ (volume day⁻¹); establishment probability ν (so transmission is β = νγ); self-reinfection fraction f; zoospore death rate μ, zoospore release per sporangium η and sporangium loss σ (day⁻¹); lethal load Smax (sporangia).

Chytridiomycosis.BriggsLoadProblem — Type
BriggsLoadProblem(p; N, S0 = zeros(N), Z0 = 0.0, tspan = (0.0, 365.0))

Deterministic Briggs et al. (2010) model for N frogs with initial loads S0 and Z0 zoospores in the pool. Each frog dies (its load is removed and frozen at 0) when its load reaches Smax; solve returns (sol, death_time, alive), with death_time[i] = NaN for survivors.

Successful transmission rate from the pool, β = νγ.

Chytridiomycosis.load_growth_rate — Method
load_growth_rate(p, N)

Asymptotic per-day growth rate of Bd load on N identical frogs (the dominant eigenvalue of the linear system). Loads grow toward Smax when it is positive and decline toward clearance when it is negative.

source
Chytridiomycosis.host_density_threshold — Function
host_density_threshold(p) -> N*

Number of frogs per pool above which Bd load grows (det J = 0): N* = -aμ / ((aγ + νγη(1-f))/V), with a = ηνf - σ. Returns 0 when a ≥ 0 (loads grow from self-reinfection alone) and Inf when no density suffices.

Chytridiomycosis.BriggsSeasonalParameters — Type
BriggsSeasonalParameters(; V, μ, η, σ, adult, tadpole = nothing, R, K, θZ, m = 0.0,
                         εZ = 0.0, summer = 100.0)
BriggsSeasonalParameters(:briggs2010; set = "fig3b" | "fig3d" | "fig3f")

Parameters of the stochastic seasonal model. Without a tadpole stage, each frog leaves Poisson(R) recruits each spring, up to the ceiling K. Chytridiomycosis.BriggsStagedProblem — Type
BriggsStagedProblem(p::BriggsLifeStages; years = 50, invade_year = 20, S0 = 10.0,
                    Δt = 1.0, poisson_below = 100.0, init = disease_free_stages(p),
                    Z0 = 0.0, record_year = nothing)

The six-stage stochastic seasonal model of Briggs et al. (2010, Fig. 4). The population starts Bd-free with init hosts per stage (rounded); at the start of summer invade_year one random adult (or, if there are none, a host of the oldest stage present) acquires S0 sporangia.

solve(prob; rng) returns a NamedTuple with end-of-summer yearly series: counts and mean_load (each a NamedTuple of vectors over the six stages; the mean is over all hosts of the stage, as in Fig. 4's "Ave. Sᵢ"), deaths (Bd deaths per stage that summer), Z, plus extinct_year (first year with no hosts at the end of summer, or nothing), persisted (hosts and Bd both present at the end) and, if record_year is set, within: daily Z, count and mean_load (days × 6 matrices) and max_load during that summer.

Chytridiomycosis.MitchellPondProblem — Type

Solving

Every problem is solved with solve (from CommonSolve.jl). Those that lower to one ODE can also be converted directly:

Chytridiomycosis.to_ode_problem — Function
to_ode_problem(prob)

The SciMLBase.ODEProblem that solve(prob) integrates, for the problems that lower to a single ODE: SchmidtSLIProblem, BriggsLoadProblem, LoadTippingProblem, BsalLarvalProblem, MultiHostPoolProblem (with its inoculation callback), ZoosporePersistenceProblem, RefugiaODEProblem, SeasonalBsalProblem (with its inflow switch times as tstops), TransmissionSLIProblem, SusceptibleChainProblem, CohabitationProblem, DrawertODEProblem and BsalStageTemperatureProblem. Use it to solve with other options or to pass the model to other SciML tools.
