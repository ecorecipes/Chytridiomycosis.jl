Midwife toads and a zoospore pool (Doddington et al. 2013)

This is a reference implementation of the model of Mallorcan midwife toads (Alytes muletensis) and free-living Bd zoospores of Doddington et al. (2013), Ecology 94:1795–1804 (doi:10.1890/12-1270.1). The equations (B.1–B.8) and parameters (Table B1) are in Appendix B; the stochastic model that produced the paper's results is the authors' Berkeley Madonna code (Supplement 1). Both are on figshare (doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.c.3306075, CC0) and were checked against the code here, together with the published version of the paper.

Time is in years, and zoospores are numbers in one pond. Tadpoles hatch from adults in the breeding season, metamorphose in summer or overwinter, and juveniles mature the year after metamorphosis. Zoospores infect tadpoles and overwintering tadpoles; infected tadpoles shed zoospores; Bd kills only juveniles, at the extra rate α; infected juveniles mature into uninfected adults.

dA/dt  = (1 − A/K) εJ (JX + JY) − μA A
dLX/dt = ψ A − (μL + εL + ν Z) LX           (L tadpoles, O overwintering tadpoles)
dOX/dt = − (μO + εO + ν Z) OX               (+ LX at time of year tM2)
dJX/dt = εL LX + εO OX − (μJ + εJ) JX
dLY/dt = ν Z LX − (μL + εL) LY
dOY/dt = ν Z OX − (μO + εO) OY              (+ LY at tM2)
dJY/dt = εL LY + εO OY − (μJ + α + εJ) JY
dZ/dt  = ρ (LY + OY) − μZ Z

ψ, εL and εO are nonzero only in their seasonal windows. Both problems use the code's 14 states, which split juveniles by age (this year's, and those able to mature) and by the kind of tadpole they came from.

Printed versus coded

The code matures juveniles at εL only during the tadpole metamorphosis window, and those from overwintering tadpoles at εO during theirs; Table B1 gives one constant εJ = 12.3 yr⁻¹. maturation = :as_coded (default) follows the code, maturation = :as_printed the table. The code's switch times (overwintering, juvenile ageing, Bd introduction) use strict inequalities that no time on its grid satisfies exactly; here each fires on the first step at or after its time.

Results

  • Exposure trial (Table 1). The group death rates and the isolate-specific excess rates with their 95% intervals are reproduced from the counts, except that the BdGPL rates come out 3.37 and 2.97 (published 3.38 and 2.98; Table B1 itself uses 2.97).
  • Fig. 3. The stochastic model gives the pattern of Fig. 3: with high virulence (α = 4.54) host survival over 35 years falls to about 0.1 at the largest ν and ρ and is 1 at small values; with weak virulence (α = 0.61) it stays above about 0.75. The half-survival contour of Fig. 3a lies at about 2–3 times larger ν than the printed shading suggests. scripts/doddington2013_fig3.jl maps the full grid.
  • Most likely outcomes. With α = 4.54 Bd goes extinct after the epizootic, as published. With α = 0.61 or α = 0 at the Table B1 values of ν and ρ, Bd extinction is the most frequent outcome here, whereas the paper reports coexistence; coexistence is the modal outcome in about half of the Fig. 3b grid (the larger ν and ρ). These targets are recorded as discrepant.
  • Not implemented. The SIR model with temperature-dependent recovery fitted in Fig. 4 (the temperature series and counts are not deposited, and the recovery link is not printed), the fungal-load individual-based model (no code) and the state-space trend model of Appendix A.
Chytridiomycosis.DoddingtonParameters — Type
DoddingtonParameters(; tB1, tB2, tM1, tM2, tOM, tAge, ψ, εL, εO, εJ, μL, μO, μJ, μA, K,
                     ρ, ν, μZ, α)
DoddingtonParameters(:doddington2013; set = "", timing = :code, kw...)

Parameters of the Doddington et al. (2013) model of Mallorcan midwife toads and Bd zoospores in one pond (Appendix B, Table B1). Rates are per year.

  • Timing, as fractions of the year: tB1–tB2 breeding season, tM1–tM2 tadpole metamorphosis, tOM start of overwintering-tadpole metamorphosis, tAge the time of year at which last year's juveniles become able to mature.
  • Host: ψ tadpoles per adult (breeding season), εL, εO, εJ metamorphosis and maturation rates, μL, μO, μJ, μA natural death rates, K the adult population limit in the density dependence 1 - A/K.
  • Pathogen: ρ zoospores released per infected tadpole, ν infection rate per zoospore in the pond, μZ zoospore death rate, α infection-specific death rate of juveniles.

The keyed constructor reads Table B1 from the compendium. set selects a figure's values ("fig3a", "fig3b", "appB_a", "appB_b", "appB_c"); keywords override single fields (for example ν and ρ for a cell of Fig. 3). timing = :code takes the code's fractions of the year (0.249, …); timing = :table takes Table B1's days of the year divided by 365. tAge is in the code only.

Chytridiomycosis.DoddingtonODEProblem — Type
DoddingtonODEProblem(p::DoddingtonParameters; A0, Z_intro, t_intro, tspan,
                     maturation = :as_coded, u0 = nothing)

The deterministic model of Doddington et al. (2013), Appendix B eqs B.1–B.8, in years, on the 14 states [A, LX, OX, JX, JMX, OJX, OJMX, LY, OY, JY, JMY, OJY, OJMY, Z] of the authors' code (juveniles are split by age and by tadpole type; see the source header). The jumps are callbacks: at time of year tM2 tadpoles become overwintering tadpoles (the δ terms); at tAge last year's juveniles become able to mature; at t_intro the zoospore count is set to Z_intro.

A0, Z_intro, t_intro and tspan = (0, t_end) default to the settings of the authors' code (150 adults, 20 000 zoospores at 15.42 yr, 50 yr) from the compendium. maturation is :as_coded (the code's seasonal maturation) or :as_printed (constant εJ). The paper's results come from the stochastic version, DoddingtonStochasticProblem.

Chytridiomycosis.DoddingtonStochasticProblem — Type
DoddingtonStochasticProblem(p::DoddingtonParameters; A0, Z_intro, t_intro, tspan, dt,
                            maturation = :as_coded, saveat = 0.01)

The stochastic model of Doddington et al. (2013): a line-by-line port of the authors' Berkeley Madonna code (Supplement 1). Each step of length dt (0.0005 yr) draws every event with a binomial (or, for zoospore release, Poisson) distribution, as the code does. Competing exits (death, metamorphosis, infection) are split with nested binomials in the code's order. States are integers, in the order of DoddingtonODEProblem.

solve(prob; rng) returns a named tuple (t, u, outcome): save times, the state at each, and the outcome at the end of the run (see doddington_outcome). Defaults come from the code: 150 adults, 20 000 zoospores introduced at 15.42 yr, a run of 50 yr. See the source header for how the switch times are handled.

Chytridiomycosis.doddington_outcome — Function
doddington_outcome(u) -> Symbol

The outcome of a run of Doddington et al. (2013) with final state u: :host_extinction when no toads are left (adults, tadpoles or juveniles), :Bd_extinction when toads remain but there are no zoospores and no infected tadpoles, and :coexistence otherwise.

source
Chytridiomycosis.doddington_outcomes — Function
doddington_outcomes(p::DoddingtonParameters; runs, rng, kwargs...) -> NamedTuple

Run DoddingtonStochasticProblem runs times (default 500, as in the paper) and count the outcomes: (; host_extinction, Bd_extinction, coexistence, runs, host_survival), where host_survival is the fraction of runs with toads left at the end (the quantity mapped in Fig. 3). Other keywords go to the problem constructor.

Chytridiomycosis.doddington_grid — Function
doddington_grid(key = :doddington2013; n = 10) -> (; ν, ρ)

The axes of Fig. 3 of Doddington et al. (2013): n log-spaced values of the transmission parameter ν and of the zoospore release rate ρ between the ends of their Table B1 ranges (2.57e-6–7.27e-5 and 1e4–6.6e6). With n = 10 they are the printed tick labels.

Chytridiomycosis.excess_death_rate — Function
excess_death_rate(deaths, time, deaths0, time0; z = 1.96) -> (; rate, lo, hi)

The isolate-specific death rate of Doddington et al. (2013), Table 1: the death rate deaths/time of an exposed group minus that of the control group, with a confidence interval from the standard error of a difference in rates, sqrt(deaths/time² + deaths0/time0²).

Table 1 of Doddington et al. (2013) recomputed from its counts in the compendium: group death rates (control, CAPE, GPL, per year) and the isolate-specific excess death rates with 95% intervals (CAPE_excess, GPL_excess, from excess_death_rate).
