Load distributions and extinction risk (Louca 2014)

Louca, Lampo and Doebeli (2014, Proc R Soc B 281:20132783, doi:10.1098/rspb.2013.2783) built a generic, continuous-time model of Bd in a frog population with tadpoles and adults. It tracks the whole distribution of the degree of infection (DOI, zoospores on a host) in each stage, and a pool of free zoospores. They derived a basic reproduction quotient Q_o, sampled a 25-dimensional parameter space and ranked the parameters by their "risk effect" on host extinction.

The implementation was checked against the paper and its electronic supplementary material (ESM), which the first author's site appends to the article (the journal's ESM link returned HTTP 403). No code or data were deposited. Time is in days and a year is Y = 365 days.

The model

$P_s(t, n)$ is the number of hosts of stage s (A adults, T tadpoles) with DOI n at time t, and Z(t) the free zoospores. A zoospore that meets a host raises its DOI by one; the within-host dynamics move the DOI up or down one at a time (main eqs 2.1–2.2, ESM eqs 1–3):

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{dP_A(n)}{dt} &= [\beta_A Z + I_{A,u}(n-1)]P_A(n-1) + I_{A,d}(n+1)P_A(n+1) - [\beta_A Z + I_{A,u}(n) + I_{A,d}(n)]P_A(n) \\ &\quad - [\mu_A(N_A) + g_A(n)]P_A(n) + \frac{\delta_{n0}}{\tau_m}\sum_{n'}\sigma_m(n')P_T(n'),\\ \frac{dP_T(n)}{dt} &= (\text{the same transitions}) - \Big[\mu_T(N_T) + g_T(n) + \frac{1}{\tau_m}\Big]P_T(n) + \delta_{n0}\, r_{br}(t)\sum_{n'}\sigma_{br}(n')P_A(n'),\\ \frac{dZ}{dt} &= \sum_s \eta_s N_s S_s - \mu_Z (Z - Z_o). \end{aligned}\]

N_s is the number of hosts and S_s their mean DOI. The functional responses are those of ESM §1.4:

  • Within-host growth (eq. 7): I_u(n) = 2 max(0, μ_S + λ)(√(n+1) − 1) and I_d(n) = μ_S n − 2 min(0, μ_S + λ)(√(n+1) − 1), so I′(0) = λ and, for λ > 0, infections settle at the within-host capacity K_S = 4λ(λ + μ_S)/μ_S² (louca_load_rates, louca_within_host_capacity).
  • Disease mortality (eq. 6): g_s(n) = (F_s − T_s) ln 2/(Y T_s) · n/(F_s − n), which halves yearly survival at the tolerance T_s and kills at the lethal DOI F_s = 10⁴.
  • Fecundity and metamorphosis: σ_br(n) = 1/(1 + n/T_br), σ_m(n) = 1/(1 + n/T_m).
  • Demography (eqs 5, 9): μ_s(N) = −ln σ_s/Y + c_s N_s, with c_s chosen so that the disease-free equilibrium under constant breeding is (K_A, K_T) (louca_competition).
  • Breeding (eq. 4): a wrapped Cauchy pulse centred on t = 0 that integrates to r a year, with full width at half maximum τ_br (louca_breeding_rate).
  • Transmission: β_s(t) = β̄_s [1 + b cos(2π(t − φ)/Y)].

Tolerant tadpoles (ESM §1.3, Table S1) have T_T = F_T, so g_T ≡ 0, and a DOI capped at S_T,max = F_T − 1. Table 1 of the paper writes this case as F_T = ∞; we follow the ESM.

Numerics

As in ESM §5, LoucaDOIProblem splits the DOIs 0 … F_s into m = 100 bins of width F_s/m and divides every transition rate by the bin width. A host dies when its DOI would reach F_s. Each run starts from a healthy population at (K_A, K_T); after 1.5 years one adult with DOI 10³ is added; the run ends after 10 years, or earlier when hosts fall below 2 (extinction) or free plus within-host zoospores fall below 10 (clearance). The paper does not say when an environmental reservoir starts. By default (reservoir_onset = :exposure) its supply μ_Z Z_o starts at exposure, so the population is healthy until the second year, as §2b describes; reservoir_onset = :start puts Z(0) = Z_o.

solve(prob; method = :ode) integrates the method-of-lines to_ode_problem with Tsit5. The default method = :semi_implicit is a fixed-step scheme: backward Euler in the DOI transitions and losses, density dependence and recruitment from the previous step, and an exact update of Z. It stays positive and stable when the reservoir makes the infection rates huge (Z_o up to 10¹⁵), where an explicit solver needs very small steps. The authors used an explicit second-order Runge–Kutta scheme with dt = 10⁻³–10⁻² d. For the Table S1 parameter sets the two methods agree to within 1%; in the Monte Carlo study the outcome with dt = 0.05 d matched dt = 0.01 d in all 800 runs checked.

With stochastic = true the base mortality becomes random disturbances (ESM §5.3) at rate μ_s(0)/f_s, each killing Binomial(⌊N_s⌋, f_s) hosts of the stage.

Invasion: the reproduction quotient

Linearising a mean-field version of the model about the disease-free state gives (main eq. 3.1; ESM eqs 18–19)

\[Q_o = \frac{1}{\bar\mu_Z}\sum_s \frac{\eta_s \bar\beta_s \bar N_s}{\bar R_s - \lambda_s},\]

the zoospores released into the water that descend from one free zoospore. R̄_s is the per-capita recruitment (turnover) rate of stage s. reproduction_quotient uses the paper's approximations N̄_s ≈ K_s, R̄_A ≈ K_T/(K_A τ_m), R̄_T ≈ (r/Y) K_A/K_T (ESM §5.2) and returns Inf when λ_s ≥ R̄_s for some stage. louca_jacobian is the averaged Jacobian J̄_o of ESM eq. (18), and louca_growth_rate its spectral bound or the approximations of eqs (21) and (23). Over sampled parameter sets the spectral bound is positive exactly when Q_o > 1.

Q_o ignores the on-host loss rate μ_S, because the mean-field model linearises the within-host dynamics as I(S) ≈ λS with λ = I′(0). In the binned model a newly infected host sits at DOI 100 (one bin), where I(n) is far below λn when μ_S is large.

Monte Carlo assessment of extinction risk

louca_sample draws a parameter set uniformly on each parameter's scale over the Table 1 ranges (louca_ranges; log-uniform for r, Z_o, μ_Z, μ_S,s, K_s, β_s, T_A, T_T, T_m, T_br) and rejects sets with a non-positive competition coefficient. louca_monte_carlo runs the exposure experiment for many draws, and louca_risk_effects computes the risk effect of each parameter: the slope of a least-squares line of the extinction indicator on the parameter (on its sampling scale), times the parameter's range. louca_outcome_probabilities bins the outcomes by log10 Q_o (Fig. 5) or by a parameter.

scripts/louca2014_montecarlo.jl runs 2000 draws per scenario (the paper ran 10⁴) and stores the summary in data/louca2014/reproduction.toml (load_louca2014).

What reproduces:

  • More extinctions occur when tadpoles suffer than when they tolerate.
  • Q_o is the best predictor without a reservoir, followed by β̄_A and μ_Z.
  • Without a reservoir, Bd clears below Q_o ≈ 0.1, persists most often near Q_o ≈ 1, and extinction dominates above 10 (Fig. 5c,d).
  • Extinction concentrates at high β̄_A and low μ_Z, and clearance at the opposite corner (Fig. 4c).

What does not reproduce:

  • The paper finds that host vulnerability (T_A, T_T) and the carrying capacities matter. In our runs they hardly do: extinctions are mostly fast collapses that run hosts to the lethal DOI whatever their tolerance.

None of these explain the gap:

  • Step size. 800/800 outcomes are equal at dt = 0.01 d.
  • Number of DOI bins. With m = 1000, extinction is 22.3% against 21.0%, and the risk effects are unchanged (scripts/louca2014_hypothesis_bins.jl).
  • Which parameter is log-uniform. The ESM lists r twice; whether the second is T_br, τ_m or τ_br does not matter (scripts/louca2014_hypothesis_scale.jl).
  • When the reservoir starts. It makes no difference whether the reservoir starts at exposure or at t = 0.

No other explanation could be tested, because the authors' C++ code was not deposited. The registry lists each comparison (louca2014_* in data/validation/targets.toml).

Parameter estimates (ESM §4)

  • louca_within_host_loss: μS ≈ 0.25 d⁻¹ from a logistic fit (λ = 0.38 d⁻¹, KS = 14.9) to infected Dendropsophus meridensis (unpublished data).
  • louca_tolerance_from_slope: T ≈ 1.73 from the fitted mortality slope g′(0) = 0.0011; louca_survival_curve is the survival curve of ESM eq. (31).
  • louca_transmission_from_growth: β̃ between 7 × 10⁻⁶ and 2 × 10⁻³ d⁻¹ from the growth of R. muscosa epizootics (ρ ≈ 0.15 d⁻¹).
  • louca_endemic_transmission: β_A between 2 × 10⁻⁷ and 3 × 10⁻⁵ d⁻¹ for the endemic Atelopus cruciger population (ESM eq. 25).

All four, and the zoospore release and loss rates the ESM takes from Woodhams et al. (2008), reproduce (validation targets louca2014_*).

Relation to the other load models

  • Briggs et al. (2010) (BriggsLoadProblem) follow the mean sporangial load of each host class. Loads grow by self-reinfection, without a within-host ceiling, until a lethal load Smax = 10⁴ sporangia. Louca's DOI is zoospores (read as ZE per swab), the lethal DOI is also 10⁴, but on a different basis: parameter_load(:louca2014, :FA) equals Vredenburg's 10⁴ ZE crash load (parameter_load) and refuses comparison with Briggs' Smax. Louca's within-host growth is sub-exponential (√n, colonial growth on the perimeter of a localised infection) and levels off at K_S. Briggs' pool loses zoospores when they infect (−γ N Z); Louca's pool has no depletion term, so β plays the part of Briggs' γ ν without the loss.
  • Drawert et al. (2017) (DrawertIBMProblem) make the Briggs model individual-based: a whole-number load per frog, like Louca's DOI, but with Briggs' exponential growth and demographic stochasticity in every event. Louca's stochastic version keeps the deterministic DOI distribution and makes only host mortality random.
  • Wilber et al. (2016) (BdIPMProblem) use a discrete-time integral projection model over log load with fitted growth, survival and loss kernels. Louca's binned master equation is its continuous-time analogue on a linear DOI grid: bins of width 100 zoospores resolve high loads, where death happens, but merge every DOI below 100 into one bin.
  • The zoospore pool (ZoosporePool) is the same linear dZ/dt = shedding − μ_Z Z in all of these; Louca adds an external reservoir Z_o.

Parameters and problem

Chytridiomycosis.LoucaParameters — Type
LoucaParameters(; Y = 365, Zo = 0, μZ, μSA, μST, λA, λT, βA, βT, φ = 0, b = 0, ηA, ηT,
                FA, FT, TA, TT, STmax, Tm, Tbr, r, τm, τbr, σA, σT, KA, KT,
                fA = 0.2, fT = 0.2, m = 100)
LoucaParameters(:louca2014; set = "", kwargs...)

Parameters of the Louca et al. (2014) model. Rates are per day, DOIs (loads) in zoospores per host (ZE per standardised swab in the paper's reading). A suffix A is the adult value and T the tadpole value.

  • Y: days in a year; Zo: environmental reservoir (zoospores);
  • μZ: free zoospore loss rate; μSA, μST: asymptotic on-host zoospore loss rates;
  • λA, λT: early within-host growth rates I′(0) (negative: the infection decays);
  • βA, βT: mean pool-to-host transmission rates (zoospore⁻¹ day⁻¹); b, φ: relative amplitude and phase lag (days) of their annual oscillation;
  • ηA, ηT: zoospores released per zoospore on a host per day;
  • FA, FT: lethal DOIs; TA, TT: DOI tolerances (yearly survival halved); STmax: maximum tadpole DOI (FT − 1 for tolerant tadpoles, which have TT = FT, or FT); Tm, Tbr: DOIs at which metamorphosis success and fecundity are halved;
  • r: yearly fecundity; τm: expected tadpole lifetime; τbr: breeding period (full width at half maximum of the breeding function; τbr ≥ Y/2 is constant breeding);
  • σA, σT: yearly survivorships without disease or crowding; KA, KT: carrying capacities, which set the competition coefficients (louca_competition);
  • fA, fT: fraction killed by one disturbance (stochastic model);
  • m: number of DOI bins.

The compendium form reads ESM Table S1 from data/compendium/louca2014.toml; set is one of "fig2ab", "fig2cd", "fig3a" and "fig3b". Keyword arguments override single values.

Chytridiomycosis.LoucaDOIProblem — Type
LoucaDOIProblem(p::LoucaParameters; years = 10, exposure_time = 1.5 Y,
                exposure_doi = 1000, stochastic = false, saveat = 1.0,
                save_distributions = false, u0 = nothing,
                reservoir_onset = :exposure)

The DOI-distribution model of Louca et al. (2014) (main eqs 2.1–2.2, ESM eqs 1–3), binned as in ESM §5: p.m bins of width F_s/m per stage, transition rates divided by the bin width, a host dies when its DOI would reach F_s, tolerant tadpoles stay in the top bin. The state is [P_A; P_T; Z] (hosts per bin, then free zoospores).

The run starts from a healthy population at (K_A, K_T) (or u0) and no free zoospores; at exposure_time one adult with DOI exposure_doi (rounded to a bin) is added. With reservoir_onset = :exposure (default) the reservoir supply μ_Z Z_o also starts then, so the population is first exposed in the second year (§2b); :start has the reservoir from t = 0 with Z(0) = Z_o, which infects the population during the burn-in. The paper does not say which it used. It ends at years years, when tadpoles plus adults fall below 2 (host extinction) or, after exposure, when free plus within-host zoospores fall below 10 (Bd clearance). Horizon, exposure and thresholds come from the compendium (ESM §5).

With stochastic = true, base mortality is replaced by random disturbances (ESM §5.3): at rate μ_s(0)/f_s, Binomial(⌊N_s⌋, f_s) hosts of stage s die, every bin scaled alike.

solve(prob; method = :semi_implicit, dt = 0.05, rng) integrates with a fixed-step scheme (backward Euler in the DOI transitions and losses, density dependence and recruitment from the previous step, exact update of Z), which stays positive when Z is large; disturbances happen in a step with probability dt μ_s(0)/f_s, as in the authors' code. method = :ode solves to_ode_problem with alg (default Tsit5()); the stochastic version then draws Poisson disturbance times in advance. Both return a named tuple:

  • outcome (:extinction, :clearance or :persistence) and t_end;
  • t, NA, NT (hosts), SA, ST (mean DOI), prevalence_A, prevalence_T (fraction with DOI > 0), Z at the save times;
  • doi: the DOI of each bin per stage, PA, PT: the final distributions, and with save_distributions = true, PA_t, PT_t: m × length(t) matrices.

Chytridiomycosis.to_ode_problem — Method
to_ode_problem(prob::LoucaDOIProblem; rng = Random.default_rng()) -> ODEProblem

The method-of-lines ODEProblem of the binned master equations, with callbacks for the exposure, the end of the run (extinction or clearance) and, for the stochastic model, the disturbances at Poisson times drawn with rng. The callbacks keep state (the next disturbance, the outcome in ode.p.status), so lower afresh for each solve.

r_br(t) = (r/Y) sinh x / (cosh x − cos(2πt/Y)), x from louca_breeding_shape.

source
Chytridiomycosis.louca_disease_mortality — Function
louca_disease_mortality(p::LoucaParameters, stage, n) -> g_s(n)

Bd-induced mortality (day⁻¹) at DOI n (ESM eq. 6): (F_s − T_s) ln2/(Y T_s) · n/(F_s − n), so that yearly survival is halved at n = T_s (exp(−Y g_s(T_s)) = 1/2) and death is certain at n = F_s. Zero for tolerant tadpoles (T_T = F_T).

source
Chytridiomycosis.louca_load_rates — Function
louca_load_rates(p::LoucaParameters, stage, n) -> (up, down)

Within-host transition rates (ESM eq. 7) from DOI n to n + 1 (up, I_u) and to n − 1 (down, I_d), with a = μ_S + λ:

I_u(n) = 2 max(0, a)(√(n+1) − 1), I_d(n) = μ_S n − 2 min(0, a)(√(n+1) − 1).

Colonial growth on the perimeter of a localised infection gives the √n terms; μ_S n is the intrinsic zoospore loss. I′(0) = λ.

source
Chytridiomycosis.louca_load_speed — Function
louca_load_speed(p::LoucaParameters, stage, n) -> I_s(n)

The expected DOI growth speed I_u(n) − I_d(n) of an individual with DOI n without reinfection (ESM Fig. S1b).

source
Chytridiomycosis.louca_within_host_capacity — Function
louca_within_host_capacity(λ, μS) -> K_S
louca_within_host_capacity(p::LoucaParameters, stage) -> K_S

The DOI at which I(n) = 0 for λ > 0 (ESM §1.4): K_S = 4λ(λ + μ_S)/μ_S². Infections grow below it and decay above it; if K_S ≥ F_s they run to the lethal DOI. 0 for λ ≤ 0.

source
Chytridiomycosis.louca_within_host_loss — Function
louca_within_host_loss(λ, KS) -> μ_S

The asymptotic on-host loss rate that gives within-host capacity KS at early growth rate λ > 0: the positive root of KS μ² = 4λ(λ + μ) (the inverse of louca_within_host_capacity). ESM §4.1 adopts λ = 0.38 d⁻¹ and KS = 14.9 from a logistic fit to D. meridensis and obtains μS ≈ 0.25 d⁻¹.

source
Chytridiomycosis.louca_competition — Function
louca_competition(p::LoucaParameters) -> (; cA, cT)

The competition coefficients of the density-dependent mortality μ_s(N) = μ_s(0) + c_s N_s that put the disease-free equilibrium with constant breeding at (K_A, K_T) (ESM eq. 9):

c_A = [σ_m(0) K_T/(τ_m K_A) − μ_A(0)]/K_A, c_T = [(r/Y) σ_br(0) K_A/K_T − 1/τ_m − μ_T(0)]/K_T.

The parameter set is valid only if both are positive (ESM §5.2).

source
Chytridiomycosis.louca_turnover — Function
louca_turnover(p::LoucaParameters) -> (; RA, RT)

Per-capita recruitment rates of the disease-free community, approximated as in ESM §5.2 from the carrying capacities: R̄_A ≈ σ_m(0) K_T/(K_A τ_m) and R̄_T ≈ (r/Y) K_A/K_T.

Invasion and estimates

Chytridiomycosis.reproduction_quotient — Function
reproduction_quotient(p::LoucaParameters) -> Q_o
reproduction_quotient(; μZ, η, β, N, R, λ) -> Q_o

The basic reproduction quotient of Bd (main eq. 3.1; ESM eq. 19): the zoospores released into the water that descend from one free zoospore early in the invasion,

Q_o = (1/μ̄_Z) Σ_s η_s β̄_s N̄_s/(R̄_s − λ_s).

For a LoucaParameters, N̄_s ≈ K_s and R̄_s from louca_turnover (ESM §5.2). The keyword form takes vectors over host types (several species and stages, ESM §2). Inf if λ_s ≥ R̄_s for some type: within-host growth outpaces turnover and Bd invades whatever the pool does.

source
Chytridiomycosis.louca_jacobian — Function
louca_jacobian(p::LoucaParameters) -> Matrix

The time-averaged Jacobian J̄_o of the mean-field model at the disease-free state (ESM eq. 18), in the variables (mean DOI of adults, of tadpoles, free zoospores):

[λ_A − R̄_A   0           β̄_A
 0           λ_T − R̄_T   β̄_T
 η_A N̄_A     η_T N̄_T     −μ̄_Z]

with N̄_s ≈ K_s and R̄_s from louca_turnover. Its spectral bound (louca_growth_rate) is positive exactly when Q_o > 1.

source
Chytridiomycosis.louca_growth_rate — Function
louca_growth_rate(p::LoucaParameters; method = :eigen) -> ρ

The early exponential growth rate of Bd after exposure (ESM §2.3):

  • :eigen: the spectral bound of louca_jacobian;
  • :leading: ρ_o, the positive root of (ρ + μ_Z)ρ = Σ η_s β̄_s N̄_s (ESM eq. 21), which ignores within-host dynamics and turnover;
  • :asymptotic: the two-term approximation of ESM eq. (23), ρ_o + Σ η_s β̄_s (λ_s − R̄_s) N̄_s / (ρ_o² + Σ η_s β̄_s N̄_s), for |λ_s − R̄_s| ≪ ρ.
source
Chytridiomycosis.louca_tolerance_from_slope — Function
louca_tolerance_from_slope(g′0; Y = 365) -> T

The DOI tolerance implied by a mortality that rises linearly in DOI with slope g′(0) (day⁻¹ per zoospore) when F ≫ T in ESM eq. (6): T = ln 2/(Y g′(0)). ESM §4.1 fits g′(0) = 0.0011 to D. meridensis survival and obtains T ≈ 1.73.

source
Chytridiomycosis.louca_survival_curve — Function
louca_survival_curve(T; xo, KS, λ, g′0, μo = 0) -> σ(T)

Survival to T days after exposure of hosts whose mean DOI grows logistically from xo with rate λ to capacity KS, under a mortality g′0 · DOI plus base mortality μo (ESM eq. 31):

σ(T) = [1 + (xo/KS)(e^{λT} − 1)]^{−KS g′0/λ} e^{−μo T}.

Chytridiomycosis.louca_ranges — Function
louca_ranges(; tadpoles = :suffer, reservoir = false, log_params) -> NamedTuple

The sampling range (lo, hi, log) of each sampled parameter (Table 1; scales from ESM §5.2), in days, for one of the paper's four scenarios: tadpoles that :suffer or :tolerate infection, with (reservoir = true, Z_o from 10 to 10¹⁵) or without (Z_o = 0) an environmental reservoir. Fixed parameters (Zo without a reservoir, TT for tolerant tadpoles) are omitted. log = true marks log-uniform sampling, for the parameters in log_params. ESM §5.2 lists r, Z_o, μ_Z, μ_S,A, μ_S,T, K_A, K_T, β_A, β_T, T_A, T_T, T_m, r; the second r is read as T_br (the default).

Chytridiomycosis.louca_sample — Function
louca_sample(rng; tadpoles = :suffer, reservoir = false, maxtries = 10_000, log_params)
    -> (; p, u)

One parameter set drawn as in ESM §5.2: each parameter of louca_ranges uniform on its (log or linear) scale, rejected and redrawn until both competition coefficients of louca_competition are positive. The fixed values (FA, FT, fA, fT, m, Y) come from the compendium. Tolerant tadpoles get TT = FT and STmax = FT − 1; suffering ones STmax = FT. Returns the parameters p and the scaled draws u (each in [0, 1], on the sampling scale).

Chytridiomycosis.louca_monte_carlo — Function
louca_monte_carlo(n; tadpoles = :suffer, reservoir = false, rng, stochastic = false,
                  dt = 0.05, years = 10, reservoir_onset = :exposure) -> Vector{NamedTuple}

n runs of the paper's Monte Carlo assessment (§2b; ESM §5.2): each draws a parameter set with louca_sample, exposes a healthy population to one infected adult after 1.5 years and runs the LoucaDOIProblem (semi-implicit, step dt) for up to years years. Each element holds p, the scaled draws u, Qo (reproduction_quotient), outcome and t_end. The paper used n = 10⁴ per scenario.

Chytridiomycosis.louca_risk_effects — Function
louca_risk_effects(results; outcome = :extinction) -> NamedTuple

The risk effect of every sampled parameter and of Q_o (§2b; ESM §5.2): the slope A of the least-squares line of the outcome indicator (1 for outcome, else 0) on the parameter, times the parameter's range X_max − X_min. Parameters are taken on their sampling scale (log10 for log-uniform ones), so the risk effect is the slope on the scaled draw u ∈ [0, 1]. For Q_o (not sampled) the regression is on log10 Q_o over the runs with finite Q_o, scaled by its observed range.

results is the output of louca_monte_carlo, or any vector of named tuples with fields u (named tuple of scaled draws), Qo and outcome.
