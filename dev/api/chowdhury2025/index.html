Life stages and temperature (Chowdhury 2025)

Chowdhury et al. (2025, Ecol Model 501:111028, doi:10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2025.111028) combined laboratory experiments on the eastern newt (Notophthalmus viridescens) with a compartmental model of B. salamandrivorans (Bsal). They asked how temperature (6 and 14 °C) and host density change epidemics in a host whose life stages live in different habitats. Adults and larvae live in water and efts (juveniles) on land. The model was checked against the published PDF and its online supplement (Appendices A–H). No code or data were deposited.

The model

Larvae S_l are never infected. They metamorphose at rate η_j, and a fraction α survives to become efts. Efts and adults each pass through susceptible, exposed, infectious and recovered classes (S, E, I, R). Efts join the adults at rate η_a(1 − A/K), which caps the adult density near K. The rest of the efts that mature leave the system. Each habitat has its own zoospore pool: Z_j in soil and Z_a in water. Time is in days and densities are per m²:

\[\begin{aligned} \dot S_l &= \lambda A - \eta_j S_l,\\ \dot S_j &= \alpha\eta_j S_l - \beta_j \frac{S_j I_j}{N_T} - f_j(Z_j) S_j - (\delta_j + \eta_a) S_j,\\ \dot E_j &= f_j(Z_j) S_j + \beta_j \frac{S_j I_j}{N_T} - (\sigma_j + \delta_j + \eta_a) E_j,\\ \dot I_j &= \sigma_j E_j - (\gamma_j + \delta_j + \mu_j + \eta_a) I_j,\\ \dot R_j &= \gamma_j I_j - (\delta_j + \eta_a) R_j,\\ \dot S_a &= \eta_a\Big(1 - \frac{A}{K}\Big) S_j - \beta_a(A) \frac{S_a I_a}{N_A} - f_a(Z_a) S_a - \delta_a S_a,\\ \dot E_a &= f_a(Z_a) S_a + \eta_a\Big(1 - \frac{A}{K}\Big) E_j + \beta_a(A) \frac{S_a I_a}{N_A} - (\sigma_a + \delta_a) E_a,\\ \dot I_a &= \eta_a\Big(1 - \frac{A}{K}\Big) I_j + \sigma_a E_a - (\gamma_a + \delta_a + \mu_a) I_a,\\ \dot R_a &= \eta_a\Big(1 - \frac{A}{K}\Big) R_j + \gamma_a I_a - \delta_a R_a,\\ \dot Z_j &= \omega_j I_j - \xi_j Z_j, \qquad \dot Z_a = \omega_a I_a - \xi_a Z_a. \end{aligned}\]

Here A is the number of adults, N_T the number of efts and N_A = A + S_l the aquatic population. Infection comes by two routes:

  • Direct contact. Adult contacts saturate with density, β_a(A) = P_a c_a A/(χ_a + A) (Holling type II, eq. 3a). Eft contacts are rare and constant, β_j = c_j P_j (eq. 3b).
  • Zoospores. f_i(Z) = ρ_i b_i Z/(κ_i + Z) (eq. 4). ρ is the fraction of contacted spores that encyst, b a contact rate with spores, and κ the ID50.

BsalStageTemperatureProblem lowers the model to an ODEProblem. The direct terms are transmission functions. The adult default is HollingContact(P_a, c_a, χ_a) diluted by larvae (A/N_A), and the eft default is FrequencyDependent(c_j P_j). Swap either to test another contact structure.

What the paper does not say, and where it disagrees with itself

  • χ_a is not printed. At 14 °C the Fig. 1 curve, the starting β_a of Fig. 7 and the R_a column of Table 3 all give χ_a ≈ 5.9 per m². At 6 °C, Fig. 7 with Table 2's c_a = 464.77 gives χ_a ≈ 12, and these values reproduce Figs 4, 5 and 7. But the Fig. 1 curve at 6 °C and the 6 °C R_a column of Table 3 need c_a ≈ 729 and χ_a ≈ 23.3. So Table 2's c_a at 6 °C was not used for Table 3.
  • Table 3 uses a different eft contact rate. §2.2 counts 8 contacts in 14 days among 12 efts and sets c_j = 8/14 = 0.571. That is per enclosure, not per eft. Table 3's R_j needs 8/(14 × 12) = 0.0476, and also b_j = 0.005 (§6.2).
  • The eft ID50 at 6 °C is not Table 2's. Table 2 prints κ_j = 10,000 for both temperatures, but Appendix C takes only the 14 °C value from the soil experiment and the 6 °C eft value "from the literature". Fig. 5a draws that ID50 at about 3,350. It is the eft LD50 at 6 °C in the data of Carter et al. (2021): the log-probit fit that gives Table 2's adult κ_a (5,443 and 2,895) gives 3,373 for efts. With it, Table 3's 6 °C R_j reproduces; with 10,000 its environmental part is a third of Table 3's. The package uses 3,348 at 6 °C; eft_id50 = :table2 restores 10,000.
  • Spore persistence in soil. §2.3 says 25 days at 6 °C and 20 at 14 °C. Table 2 gives the reverse (ξ_j = 1/20, 1/25), and only Table 2 reproduces Table 3. Appendix C says efts mature after 3.5 years and Table 2 says 3; again only Table 2 reproduces Table 3. as_printed = true uses the text values.
  • Efts. With Table 2's birth rate (λ = 0.82 eggs per adult per day) larvae recruit several efts per m² within months, so the simulated eft numbers rise. In Figs 4, 5 and E.1 they stay flat or fall and never recover over 10 years, and they start at 80% of the stated density (0.64 for 0.8). The rate of infection from soil spores in Fig. 7 is also about ten times what the Z_j of Fig. 5 gives. One reading reproduces all the eft curves of Figs 4, 5 and 7 at both temperatures and all densities: the simulations behave as if they used λ/24, a half-saturation of κ_j/24 in f_j, and efts starting at 0.8 times the stated density. These three were found by fitting the figures; the paper prints none of them, so they are not defaults (the tests show the fit). The adult curves are the same either way.
  • Units of the pools. Z_a is compared with κ_a in spores per mL and Z_j with κ_j in spores per m², but both pools are filled by ω I with I per m². The paper equates them without a volume. The package keeps the paper's numbers.

ChowdhuryParameters(:chowdhury2025; variant = :table3) gives the values that reproduce Table 3.

Reproduction numbers

The next-generation matrix has two non-zero eigenvalues (Appendix F), one per habitat. Each is the sum of a direct and an environmental route:

\[\mathcal{R}_j = \frac{c_jP_j\sigma_j}{D_j(\eta_a + \delta_j + \sigma_j)} + \frac{\rho_j S_j^*\omega_j\sigma_j b_j}{\kappa_j\xi_j D_j(\eta_a + \delta_j + \sigma_j)}, \qquad \mathcal{R}_a = \frac{S_a^{*2}c_aP_a\sigma_a}{(\sigma_a + \delta_a)D_a(S_l^* + S_a^*)(\chi_a + S_a^*)} + \frac{\rho_a\omega_a b_a\sigma_a S_a^*}{\kappa_a\xi_a(\sigma_a + \delta_a)D_a},\]

with D_j = δ_j + η_a + γ_j + μ_j and D_a = μ_a + δ_a + γ_a. R₀ = max(R_j, R_a). The package computes them from the numerical next-generation matrix, so they also hold for other contact functions, and checks them against these formulas in the tests. The disease-free state is far from the simulated one: it has about 16 efts and 85 larvae per adult.

Sensitivity

The paper ranks parameters by Latin hypercube sampling with partial rank correlation (Fig. 8, Appendix H) and by elasticities of R_j and R_a (Appendix G). The sampling ranges are not given, so the PRCC values cannot be reproduced. stage_elasticities computes the elasticities, and their signs agree with every direction stated in §6.1.

API

Chytridiomycosis.ChowdhuryParameters — Type
ChowdhuryParameters(; λ, α, η_j, η_a, K, δ_j, δ_a, σ_j, σ_a, κ_j, κ_a, γ_j = 0,
                    γ_a = 0, μ_j, μ_a, ρ_j, ρ_a, ξ_j, ξ_a, ω_j, ω_a, P_j, P_a, b_j, b_a,
                    c_j, c_a, χ_a, temperature = NaN)
ChowdhuryParameters(:chowdhury2025; temperature = 14, K = 2, variant = :table2,
                    as_printed = false, eft_id50 = :appendix_c, kwargs...)

Parameters of the Chowdhury et al. (2025) stage-structured Bsal model of the eastern newt (Table 2). Rates are per day and densities per m².

  • Life cycle: birth rate λ (eggs per adult per day), larval survival to metamorphosis α, larva-to-eft rate η_j, eft-to-adult rate η_a, adult carrying capacity K, and natural death rates δ_j (efts) and δ_a (adults). Larvae do not die except through α.
  • Disease, eft (_j) and adult (_a): rate of leaving the exposed class σ, recovery γ (zero in the paper), Bsal-induced death μ, shedding ω (zoospores per infected host per day), spore decay ξ, encystment fraction ρ, spore contact rate b, ID50 κ (spores per m² for efts, per mL for adults) and probability of transmission per contact P.
  • Contacts: c_j, the per capita eft contact rate (constant), and the adult Holling type II contact rate c_a A/(χ_a + A) with maximum c_a and half-saturation density χ_a.

The keyed constructor reads the compendium (data/compendium/chowdhury2025.toml) at temperature 6 or 14 °C with carrying capacity K (the paper uses 2, 4, 8 and 16 m⁻², see chowdhury_scenarios). Other keywords override single fields.

  • variant = :table2 (default) uses Table 2, with χ_a derived from Fig. 7 (χ_a is not printed). These values reproduce the simulations of Figs 4, 5 and 7.
  • variant = :table3 uses the values that reproduce Table 3's reproduction numbers: c_j = 8/(14 × 12) per capita, b_j = 0.005 (§6.2) and, at 6 °C, the contact curve of Fig. 1 (c_a ≈ 729, χ_a ≈ 23.3) instead of Table 2's c_a = 464.77.
  • as_printed = true uses the values printed in the text where they contradict Table 2: terrestrial spore persistence of 25 days at 6 °C and 20 days at 14 °C (§2.3; Table 2 has the reverse), and an eft stage of 3.5 years (Appendix C; Table 2 has 3).
  • eft_id50 = :appendix_c (default) takes the eft ID50 κ_j at 6 °C as the paper used it: 3,348 per m², the ID50 line of Fig. 5a, which Appendix C says came from the literature (it is the eft LD50 at 6 °C in Carter et al. 2021) and which reproduces Table 3's 6 °C R_j. At 14 °C κ_j = 10,000 (Appendix A; Fig. 5b). eft_id50 = :table2 uses Table 2's 10,000 at both temperatures.

Chytridiomycosis.BsalStageTemperatureProblem — Type
BsalStageTemperatureProblem(p::ChowdhuryParameters; adults = p.K, efts = adults/20,
                            exposed = 0.01, larvae = 0, tspan = (0.0, 360.0),
                            adult_contact = HollingContact(p.P_a, p.c_a, p.χ_a),
                            eft_contact = FrequencyDependent(p.c_j * p.P_j),
                            larval_dilution = true, u0 = nothing)

The stage-structured Bsal model of Chowdhury et al. (2025, §3.2.3), in days, with state [S_l, S_j, E_j, I_j, R_j, S_a, E_a, I_a, R_a, Z_j, Z_a] (larvae; efts; adults; terrestrial and aquatic zoospores). solve(prob) integrates the ODEProblem of to_ode_problem with Tsit5.

The default start is the paper's (§5.1): adults and efts per m² with a fraction exposed of each in the exposed class, no larvae and no zoospores. Give u0 to start elsewhere.

The direct transmission terms can be replaced by any TransmissionFunction:

  • adult_contact gives the adult term as infection_rate(adult_contact, S_a, I_a, A), multiplied by A/N_A when larval_dilution is true (N_A = A + S_l). The default, HollingContact(P_a, c_a, χ_a) with the dilution, is the paper's β_a(A) S_a I_a/N_A with β_a(A) = P_a c_a A/(χ_a + A) (eq. 3a).
  • eft_contact gives the eft term as infection_rate(eft_contact, S_j, I_j, N_T). The default FrequencyDependent(c_j P_j) is the paper's β_j S_j I_j/N_T (eq. 3b).

For example adult_contact = FrequencyDependent(0.23) puts the Tompros et al. (2022) estimate, frequency-dependent and fitted in 1-m² tanks at 14 °C, in place of the paper's contact function (a new combination, not a published result).

Chytridiomycosis.stage_transmission_rates — Function
stage_transmission_rates(prob::BsalStageTemperatureProblem, u) -> NamedTuple

The transmission quantities of Fig. 7 at state u:

  • β_a: the adult direct transmission rate per susceptible-infectious pair, transmission_coefficient(adult_contact, A), which is P_a c_a A/(χ_a + A) (eq. 3a) for the default contact function;
  • β_j: transmission_coefficient(eft_contact, N_T), c_j P_j by default (eq. 3b);
  • f_j, f_a: the per capita rates of infection from zoospores, ρ b Z/(κ + Z) (eq. 4);
  • direct_j, direct_a, environmental_j, environmental_a: the total rates of new infections (per m² per day) by each route.

Chytridiomycosis.stage_disease_free_state — Function
stage_disease_free_state(p::ChowdhuryParameters) -> (; S_l, S_j, S_a)

The disease-free equilibrium of Appendix F (per m²):

S_a* = ((αλ − δ_a) η_a − δ_a δ_j) K / (α η_a λ),
S_l* = λ S_a*/η_j,      S_j* = α λ S_a*/(δ_j + η_a).

It exists (is positive) when α λ η_a > δ_a (η_a + δ_j). With Table 2 it has S_a* ≈ 0.98 K, S_j* ≈ 15.9 K and S_l* ≈ 85 K: far more efts and larvae than the simulations start with (0.05 K efts, no larvae).

Chytridiomycosis.stage_next_generation_matrix — Function
stage_next_generation_matrix(p::ChowdhuryParameters; adult_contact, eft_contact,
                             larval_dilution = true) -> (; F, V, K, R0)

The next-generation matrix K = F V⁻¹ at the disease-free state (stage_disease_free_state), with infected compartments (E_j, I_j, E_a, I_a, Z_j, Z_a) as in Appendix F. Shedding into the pools and the maturation of infected efts into adults are transitions (V); only infections of hosts are new (F). The direct terms are differentiated numerically, so any TransmissionFunction can be used; the defaults are those of BsalStageTemperatureProblem. R0 is the spectral radius of K.

Only the E_j and E_a rows of K are non-zero and adult infections never produce eft infections, so the eigenvalues are K[1,1] = R_j and K[3,3] = R_a (stage_reproduction_numbers).

Chytridiomycosis.stage_reproduction_numbers — Function
stage_reproduction_numbers(p::ChowdhuryParameters; kwargs...) -> NamedTuple

The two eigenvalues of the next-generation matrix (eqs. 5a, 5b; Appendix F), each split into its direct and environmental routes: R_j = direct_j + environmental_j for efts, R_a = direct_a + environmental_a for adults, and R0 = max(R_j, R_a). Keywords as for stage_next_generation_matrix. For the default contact functions,

R_j = c_j P_j σ_j / (D_j (η_a + δ_j + σ_j))
      + ρ_j S_j* ω_j σ_j b_j / (κ_j ξ_j D_j (η_a + δ_j + σ_j)),
R_a = S_a*² c_a P_a σ_a / ((σ_a + δ_a) D_a (S_l* + S_a*)(χ_a + S_a*))
      + ρ_a ω_a b_a σ_a S_a* / (κ_a ξ_a (σ_a + δ_a) D_a),

with D_j = δ_j + η_a + γ_j + μ_j and D_a = μ_a + δ_a + γ_a. Table 3 is reproduced with variant = :table3 (both columns, both temperatures; at 6 °C R_j needs the default eft_id50 = :appendix_c).

Chytridiomycosis.stage_elasticities — Function
stage_elasticities(p::ChowdhuryParameters; number = :R_a, h = 1e-6) -> Dict{Symbol,Float64}

Elasticities (θ/R) ∂R/∂θ of R_j or R_a (stage_reproduction_numbers) with respect to each parameter θ of p (Appendix G, eq. 6), by central differences in log θ. Parameters equal to zero (the recovery rates) have elasticity zero. ω_j = ω_a and P_j = P_a in the paper but are varied separately here.
