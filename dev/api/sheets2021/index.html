Thermal performance of five Bd isolates (Sheets 2021)

This is a reference implementation of the growth-curve analysis of Sheets et al. (2021), Front Vet Sci 8:687084 (doi:10.3389/fvets.2021.687084). Five BdGPL isolates from the USA (LA, TN, VT: GPL1; NM, OH: GPL2) were grown at 4, 12, 17, 21, 25, 26 and 27 °C, and their viability followed by the MTT assay. For each isolate and temperature a logistic curve is fitted to the normalised viability, by maximum likelihood with a standard deviation that grows with the mean (the authors' R Markdown, figshare doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.14714865):

x(t) = K x0 / (x0 + (K − x0) exp(−r t))
MTTn_i ~ Normal(x(t_i), b0 + 1e-4 + b1 x(t_i))

The thermal response of an isolate is then r (or K) against temperature (Fig. 4, Table 2), available as an IsolateTPC through sheets_tpc. The data (figshare doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.14714841, CC BY 4.0) are bundled in data/sheets2021.

What is and is not reproduced

The refit reproduces 22 of the 35 fits of Table 2 to its four significant figures and four more to 2 %. Five fits (TN, OH and VT at 4 °C, TN at 26 °C, OH at 25 °C) differ from Table 2; the authors' own code, run on the deposited data, gives our values, so Table 2 came from other data or another run. K is not identified at LA 26 °C (printed 107300) or VT 27 °C, and VT 26 °C lies on a ridge in (x0, r). Table 2's mean viabilities put the LA optimum at 17 °C, not the 21 °C the Results state.

The Supplementary Material (DataSheet1.PDF) is the rendered R Markdown. Its printed fits are the authors' output for the deposited data, and they equal ours (and differ from Table 2 for the five fits above). It has no Johnson–Lewin curves, although the main text refers to it for them; they are not implemented. Its profile-likelihood 95% intervals for r and K are reproduced by sheets_profile_interval (to 1 % for r, 4 % for K; the authors interpolate on a coarse grid).

Chytridiomycosis.sheets_tpc — Function
sheets_tpc(isolate; trait = :r, extrapolation = :zero) -> IsolateTPC

The thermal response of one Bd isolate of Sheets et al. (2021) ("LA", "TN", "VT", "NM" or "OH"), as an IsolateTPC that interpolates linearly (InterpolatedTPC) between the seven temperatures of Table 2. trait is

  • :r: the logistic growth rate of normalised MTT viability (day⁻¹);
  • :K: its carrying capacity (OD);
  • :MTT_mean: the mean maximum viability (OD at 570 nm);
  • :zoospore_density: the mean maximum zoospore density (Table 4; no value at 25 °C).

The values are the paper's (compendium :sheets2021, sets "<isolate>_<T>C"). The authors show (Fig. 4) that r stays high at 25–27 °C while K collapses, so r alone is a poor summary of growth at high temperature; and K at LA 26 °C (printed 107300) is not identified.

Chytridiomycosis.SheetsGrowthModel — Type
SheetsGrowthModel(isolate, T; data = load_sheets2021())

The likelihood of Sheets et al.'s (2021) logistic fit for one isolate at one temperature (°C): MTTn_i ~ Normal(μ_i, b0 + 1e-4 + b1 μ_i), with μ_i the logistic curve delayed_logistic(t_i, x0, K, r) (no delay). Parameters, all on the log scale: x0, r, K, b0, b1 (parameter_names). The model implements the LogDensityProblems interface; the log density is the log-likelihood (a flat prior on the log parameters). Fit it with maximum_likelihood.

Chytridiomycosis.sheets_fits — Function
sheets_fits(; data = load_sheets2021(), kwargs...) -> Vector{NamedTuple}

The logistic fit of every isolate at every temperature (35 fits; Sheets et al. 2021 Table 2 and Fig. 4), each a NamedTuple with isolate, T, the estimates x0, r, K, b0, b1, loglik and nobs. Keywords go to maximum_likelihood.

Chytridiomycosis.sheets_profile_interval — Function
sheets_profile_interval(fit, name::Symbol; level = 0.95, maxfactor = 20.0, iters = 1500)
    -> (lower, upper, found_lower, found_upper)

Profile-likelihood confidence interval of parameter name (:r or :K) of a maximum_likelihood fit of a SheetsGrowthModel, as in the Supplementary Material of Sheets et al. (2021): the values at which the likelihood ratio statistic 2 (ℓ̂ − ℓ_profile) reaches the level quantile of χ²₁, with the other four parameters re-optimised at each fixed value. The authors scan a grid and interpolate; this finds each crossing by bisection on log(name) between the estimate and maxfactor times (or 1/maxfactor of) it. When no crossing is found in that range the bound is returned with found_* = false (an unidentified bound, plotted dotted in the paper).

source
