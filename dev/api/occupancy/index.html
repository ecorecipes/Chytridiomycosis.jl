Occupancy models (Mosher 2018, Adams 2010)

Occupancy models estimate where a species, or a pathogen, occurs when surveys can miss it. This page has a reusable occupancy layer and two published Bd applications:

  • Mosher et al. (2018), Ecol Appl 28:926–937 (doi:10.1002/eap.1699): a dynamic two-species occupancy model of boreal toads and Bd at historic breeding sites in the southern Rocky Mountains, 2001–2010, with colonisation and extinction of each species conditional on the other and on elevation.
  • Adams et al. (2010), Ecol Appl 20:289–302 (doi:10.1890/08-2319.1): single-season occupancy of Bd in native-amphibian and bullfrog populations, where each animal tested is a survey.
What was checked
  • Mosher et al. 2018. The equations were checked against the accepted manuscript of the article, and its models were refitted to the Dryad data (doi:10.5061/dryad.1c990, CC0, bundled in data/mosher2018).
    • The fits reproduce the AICc of 10 of the 12 models in Table 3, to 0.01, and the estimates quoted in the text.
    • The deposit has 83 sites; dropping Site 33, which has no covariates, gives the paper's
    • The transition and detection matrices are written out only in the first author's dissertation (Mosher 2017, Ch. 1). The article describes them in words.
  • Adams et al. 2010. Checked against the published article. No detection histories are deposited, so the models cannot be refitted. The targets use quantities derived from the printed coefficients (Tables 4 and 6), and three of them disagree with numbers printed in the text.

Occupancy layer

Single-season occupancy (MacKenzie et al. 2002): a site is occupied with probability ψ, and each survey detects the species with probability p if it is there. Dynamic multistate occupancy is a hidden Markov model over seasons, with the surveys of a season as repeated observations of a fixed state (robust design). It uses hmm_loglikelihood and robust_design_likelihoods.

Chytridiomycosis.occupancy_loglikelihood — Function
occupancy_loglikelihood(y::AbstractVector, ψ, p) -> Real
occupancy_loglikelihood(Y::AbstractMatrix, ψ, p) -> Real

Single-season occupancy log-likelihood (MacKenzie et al. 2002). y is one site's detection history (1 detected, 0 not, missing no survey); ψ is the occupancy probability and p the detection probability, one number or one per survey. For a matrix Y (rows are sites) the site log-likelihoods are summed; ψ may then be a number or a vector over sites and p a number, a vector over sites or a matrix the size of Y. Assumes no false positives and closure within the season.

Two species: host and pathogen

The conditional parameterisation of Richmond et al. (2010) has four states: A (species A only), B (B only), U (neither) and AB (both). In Mosher et al. species A is the toad and B is Bd. With rows the state in year t and columns the state in year t + 1:

      A                B                U                AB
A     (1−εA)(1−γBA)    εA γBA           εA(1−γBA)        (1−εA)γBA
B     γAB εB           (1−γAB)(1−εB)    (1−γAB)εB        γAB(1−εB)
U     γA(1−γB)         (1−γA)γB         (1−γA)(1−γB)     γA γB
AB    (1−εAB)εBA       εAB(1−εBA)       εAB εBA          (1−εAB)(1−εBA)

In one survey, a site in state AB is recorded as AB with probability rA rBA, as A with rA(1 − rBA), as B with (1 − rA) rBa and as U otherwise. Bd is found only on swabs of toads that were found, so pB = rBa = 0. The Bd-only state is then never observed, and some parameters are poorly identified (Mosher 2017, Ch. 1).

Mosher et al. 2018: boreal toads and Bd

Mosher2018Problem projects the expected proportions of sites in each state. With the dissertation's "SRM boreal toad–Bd" scenario (compendium base set), toad occupancy falls from 0.90 to 0.71 over four years; with its rare, susceptible host it falls from 0.20 to 0.07. Both match dissertation Table 1.3, which checks the transition matrix.

Chytridiomycosis.Mosher2018Parameters — Type
Mosher2018Parameters(; ψA, ψBA, ψBa, γA, γAB, γB, γBA, εA, εAB, εB, εBA, pA, rA, rBA,
                       pB = 0, rBa = 0)
Mosher2018Parameters(:mosher2018; set = "")

Parameters of the dynamic two-species (host–pathogen) occupancy model of Mosher et al. (2018), species A the boreal toad and B Bd, all probabilities per year (vital rates) or per survey (detection):

  • ψA, ψBA, ψBa: initial occupancy of toads, and of Bd with toads present or absent;
  • γA, γAB: toad colonisation with Bd absent or present; εA, εAB: toad extinction with Bd absent or present;
  • γB, γBA: Bd colonisation with toads absent or present; εB, εBA: Bd extinction with toads absent or present;
  • pA, rA: toad detection with Bd absent or present; rBA: Bd detection on a detected toad; pB, rBa: Bd detection with toads absent or undetected (0 for swab data).

The keyed constructor reads the scenarios of Mosher (2017, PhD dissertation, Table 1.3) from the compendium: the base set is the "SRM boreal toad–Bd" scenario chosen to mimic the southern Rocky Mountain data, and set = "rare_susceptible" the rare, susceptible host. These are simulation scenarios, not estimates. The estimates of the paper come from refitting its models to the data (Mosher2018Model, mosher2018_parameters_at).

Data and refit

Mosher2018Model is the likelihood with covariates on the logit scale:

  • elevation (linear or quadratic), other amphibians, habitat permanence or region for colonisation and extinction;
  • day of year (quadratic) for toad detection;
  • elevation for Bd detection.

Initial occupancy is constant. When both members of a pair (γA/γAB, γB/γBA, εA/εAB, εB/εBA, pA/rA) have the same covariate structure, they share slopes, as the caption of Table 3 says. Fit the model with maximum_likelihood. mosher2018_models lists the 12 models printed in Table 3, and scripts/mosher2018_refit.jl fits them all in about two minutes.

The refit reproduces:

  • the AICc of 10 of the 12 printed models, and the deviance (2175.3) and K (25) of the best;
  • εA = εB = 0.00 in the all-constant model; in the best model, εA = 0.00, εB = 0.02 and γA = 1.00;
  • the elevation patterns of Fig. 5: εAB and εBA peak at about 0.3 at intermediate elevations, toads recolonise high sites (γAB increases with elevation), and Bd colonises low and mid-elevation sites.
Not reproduced
  • Model 3 (γB, γBA, εBA quadratic). Our deviance is 0.22 lower than the printed one, so the printed fit seems to fall short of the maximum.
  • The null model. Table 3 prints K = 14 and deviance 2232.15. Ours has one constant per rate pair: K = 13 and a lower deviance, 2230.93. The paper does not describe its null model further.
Choices where the paper is silent
  • AICc sample size. It is the number of site-years with at least one survey (494 at the 82 sites). The paper does not state it. This value reproduces every AICc–deviance pair in Table 3, which number of sites (82) does not.
  • Standardised covariates. This does not change the maximised likelihood.
  • Site 33 dropped. This gives the paper's 82 sites; see data/mosher2018/README.md.

Chytridiomycosis.Mosher2018Model — Type
Mosher2018Model(data::Mosher2018Data; additive = true, null = false, kw...)

The Mosher et al. (2018) likelihood for data with a covariate structure for each of the rates γA, γAB, γB, γBA, εA, εAB, εB, εBA, pA, rA, rBA, given as keywords (default :const). Each is one of :const, :elev, :elev2 (linear and quadratic in elevation), :amph, :perm, :region (site covariates) or, for pA, rA, :doy, :doy2 (day of year). Initial occupancy ψA, ψBA, ψBa is constant; pB = rBa = 0.

With additive = true, the members of a pair (γA, γAB), (γB, γBA), (εA, εAB), (εB, εBA), (pA, rA) that have the same non-constant structure share their slopes (the "additive relationships between parameter pairs" of the paper's Table 3). null = true sets each colonisation or extinction pair equal (one constant per pair), the null model in which rates do not depend on the other species. Covariates are standardised.

Implements the LogDensityProblems interface (the log-likelihood on the logit scale) and parameter_names; fit with maximum_likelihood.

Adams et al. 2010: Bd in amphibian populations

Here a "site" is a host population and a "survey" is one animal swabbed once. The probability p that an animal from a population with Bd tests positive combines prevalence and test sensitivity. Native anurans (72 populations analysed) and bullfrogs (40) were fitted separately in Program MARK, with logit-linear covariates:

  • Native anurans, best model. p depends on larvae, date, elevation, date × elevation and the human footprint index; ψ depends on Rana luteiventris.
  • Bullfrogs, best model. p depends on larvae, date and stagnant water; ψ depends on latitude.

SingleSeasonOccupancyModel is this likelihood with any design matrices. No detection histories are deposited, so the paper's models cannot be refitted. Its printed coefficients (Tables 4 and 6) are in the compendium.

From those coefficients:

  • Bullfrogs. ψ = 0.664 (CI 0.429–0.838) is reproduced, and so are all four odds ratios quoted in the text.

  • Native anurans. The R. luteiventris odds ratio (4.2) is reproduced. Three printed numbers do not follow:

    QuantityPrintedFrom Table 4
    ψ53.4%0.741 (non-R. luteiventris populations)
    larval odds ratio0.100.088
    odds ratio per human-footprint point2.371.54; 2.37 is close to 2.39, the ratio per standard deviation

Appendix A: because a population has a finite number N of animals, the probability that Bd occurs and could be detected is ψ[1 − (1 − p′)^N], not ψ. The difference is small for p′ > 0.05 and N > 50.

Chytridiomycosis.finite_population_occurrence — Function
finite_population_occurrence(ψ, p′, N)

Probability ψ[1 − (1 − p′)^N] that the pathogen occurs in a population of N animals and at least one of them could test positive, with per-animal probability p′ (Adams et al. 2010, Appendix A). Divided by ψ it is the ratio plotted in their Fig. A1, which approaches 1 as N and p′ increase.
