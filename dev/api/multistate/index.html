Multistate survival (Hollanders 2023, Palomar 2023)

Does infection status or infection intensity predict mortality in enzootic populations? Two published capture–recapture analyses answer it differently, and both are implemented here on one multistate layer built on the hidden Markov model functions.

  • Palomar, Fernández-Chacón and Bosch (2023, Biodiversity and Conservation 32:793–809, 10.1007/s10531-022-02525-3) fitted a multi-event model to 13 years of male Bufo spinosus capture histories and found lower annual survival in Bd-positive toads (0.754 against 0.883). The histories were not deposited, so the model is implemented and tested on simulated data, and its estimates are pending validation targets.
  • Hollanders, Grogan, Nock, McCallum and Newell (2023, Ecological Applications 33:e2724, 10.1002/eap.2724) fitted a continuous-time robust-design multistate model to Mixophyes fleayi at three streams. Infection status had no effect on mortality, and swab load did. Their data are on Dryad under CC0 and are bundled (data/hollanders2023/); their code is on Zenodo; their posterior draws come from their repository and are not bundled.

The multistate layer

Every individual's capture history is one HMM sequence, conditioned on its first capture. There are K alive states and an absorbing dead state. Events are coded 0 (not seen) and 1…E (seen, with whatever the field record says); missing marks an occasion without a survey.

  • Discrete time. Survival S comes first, then movement between states Ψ (survival_transition). Detection p comes next, then state assignment δ (multievent_emission), as in E-SURGE's S, T, B1 and B2 steps.
  • Continuous time. The generator Q holds the hazards. Each interval of length τ has the transition matrix exp(Qτ) (ctmc_transition), so unequal intervals need no special treatment. For two infection states and death (infection_generator), the matrix exponential has a closed form.
  • Robust design. The state is constant within a primary occasion, and its secondary surveys are independent draws (robust_design_likelihoods).
Chytridiomycosis.ctmc_transition — Function
ctmc_transition(Q, τ) -> Matrix

Transition probability matrix exp(Qτ) of a continuous-time Markov chain with generator Q (rows sum to zero, off-diagonals are rates) over an interval of length τ in the time unit of the rates. Unequal intervals between occasions are handled by passing each one's τ (Conn et al. 2012; Glennie et al. 2022). A 3×3 generator whose last row is zero (two transient states and an absorbing state, as in infection_generator) uses a closed form that is exact and differentiable; other generators use LinearAlgebra.exp.

Chytridiomycosis.infection_generator — Function
infection_generator(gain, clearance, mortality_uninfected, mortality_infected) -> Matrix

The 3×3 generator of the infection–survival process of Hollanders et al. (2023), states (alive uninfected, alive infected, dead), all arguments hazard rates in one time unit:

[−(gain + μ_U)   gain              μ_U
 clearance       −(clearance + μ_I) μ_I
 0               0                 0  ]

Pass it to ctmc_transition with the interval length.

source
Chytridiomycosis.survival_transition — Function
survival_transition(S, Ψ) -> Matrix

Discrete-time multistate transition matrix with K = length(S) alive states and a dead state appended: survive with probability S[k], then move between alive states with the row-stochastic K×K matrix Ψ (the two-step S then T pattern of E-SURGE). Entry [k, l] is S[k] Ψ[k, l], [k, K + 1] is 1 − S[k], and the dead state is absorbing.

source
Chytridiomycosis.multievent_emission — Function
multievent_emission(p, δ) -> Matrix

Observation matrix of a multi-event model with K = length(p) alive states and a dead state: an animal in state k is encountered with probability p[k] (matrix B1 of E-SURGE), and an encountered animal gives seen-event e with probability δ[k, e] (B2; rows of δ sum to one). Column 1 is event 0 (not seen), column e + 1 is event e. The dead state is never seen. With δ the identity this is a multistate (Arnason–Schwarz) emission; with off-diagonal or "unknown" columns it carries state uncertainty (Pradel 2005).

source
Chytridiomycosis.robust_design_likelihoods — Function
robust_design_likelihoods(emission, codes) -> Matrix

The T×K emission-likelihood matrix (for hmm_forward) of one individual under the robust design: the state is constant within a primary occasion and each of its secondary surveys is an independent observation. codes is a T×J matrix of observation-column indices (missing where there was no survey); emission(t, j) returns the K×O observation matrix of secondary j of primary t (or pass one matrix for all). Row t of the result is the product over the non-missing secondaries; a primary with none is a row of ones.

Chytridiomycosis.quasistationary_distribution — Function
quasistationary_distribution(Φ) -> Vector

Long-run distribution over the alive states of a transition matrix Φ whose last state is an absorbing death: the normalised left Perron vector of the alive block (the state mix of the survivors once the initial condition is forgotten). For two states (infected, uninfected) its first entry is the infection prevalence the transition and survival estimates imply among living animals.

Chytridiomycosis.MultiEventStructure — Type
MultiEventStructure(; nstates, assignment, baseline, survival = :state,
                      transition = :state, detection = :state, state_assignment = :state)

The model pattern of a multi-event capture–recapture model (Pradel 2005) with nstates alive states and a dead state.

  • assignment is a K×E Bool matrix: assignment[k, e] is true when an encountered animal in state k can give seen-event e (events 1…E; event 0 is "not seen"). baseline[k] is the column whose probability is one minus the others (E-SURGE's *).
  • Each parameter class is logit- (or multinomial-logit-) linear with a design given by a symbol: :constant, :state, :time, :state_plus_time (additive) or :state_time (interaction). Survival S[k, t] and transitions ψ[k → l, t] vary over the T − 1 intervals, detection p[k, t] over occasions 2…T; state assignment has no time effect (:constant or :state). Transitions are a multinomial logit on each row with staying as the baseline; the initial-state probabilities π (at first capture) are free (K − 1 parameters).

The model names of Palomar et al. (2023, Table 1) map directly: "state + time" is :state_plus_time, "state * time" :state_time, "(.)" :constant.

Chytridiomycosis.MultiEventProblem — Type
MultiEventProblem(histories, structure::MultiEventStructure; unassessed = Int[])

Maximum-likelihood problem for a multi-event (or multistate CJS) model. histories is an n×T matrix of event codes (0 not seen, 1…E seen events; missing for an occasion not surveyed), one row per marked individual. Rows are conditioned on first capture; individuals never seen, or first seen on the last occasion, carry no information and are dropped, and identical histories are pooled with counts. unassessed lists occasions at which no animal's state could be assessed (for example, years without swabbing): there an encountered animal gives its state's baseline event with probability one, whatever the state-assignment parameters.

It is a LogDensityProblems target whose log density is the log-likelihood (a flat prior on the unconstrained parameters, in the order of prob.names). solve(prob) maximises it (BFGS with finite-difference gradients from x0 = zeros) and returns a MultiEventFit. multievent_parameters gives the natural-scale parameters at a parameter vector; simulate_capture_histories draws data.

The tests check the multi-event likelihood against brute-force summation over every latent path. They check the closed-form exponential against LinearAlgebra.exp. They also check that simulated histories return the parameters that generated them.

Palomar et al. 2023: multi-event survival of Bufo spinosus

The model has states I (Bd+), U (Bd−) and dead. Each year a toad survives, then may change state. A toad alive at the pond is recaptured with probability p. A recaptured toad's status is recorded with probability δ (otherwise "unknown").

The main text's event coding contradicts its own matrix B2. The text calls event 1 "Bd-negative", but B2 sends I to event 1. Online Appendix A codes 1 as infected and 2 as uninfected, which agrees with B2, so that coding is used here.

Chytridiomycosis.palomar2023_swabbing_experiment — Function
palomar2023_swabbing_experiment(; rng = Random.Xoshiro(2023), survival = 0.8,
    infection = 0.2, clearance = 0.6, recapture = 0.6, swab_fraction = 0.43,
    unswabbed = [1, 2, 12, 13], initial_infected = 0.28,
    releases = [100, 80, 70, 60, 55, 50, 45, 40, 35, 30, 25, 22, 20],
    structure = palomar2023_structure()) -> NamedTuple

A simulation experiment (our analysis, not a published result) on the design of Palomar et al. (2023). Capture histories of 632 males over 13 annual occasions are simulated with the same survival in both states, constant infection and clearance probabilities and recapture, and state assessed by swabbing a random fraction swab_fraction of captures, independent of state, except in the unswabbed years (2006, 2007, 2017 and 2018 were not swabbed; swabs were taken 2008–2016), when every capture is "status unknown". The same histories are then fitted with structure (default: the published model 1) twice: as published, with a state-dependent δ constant over years (published), and with the unswabbed years declared unassessed (unassessed). Each fit is summarised by the mean over the first T − 2 intervals (the last is confounded) of S_I, S_U, ψ_U→I, ψ_I→U, and its δ_I, δ_U; truth holds the simulated values and histories the data.

source

Two checks use only the printed numbers.

  1. Implied prevalence. The mean survival and transition probabilities imply that about 71% of living males are infected in the long run. The observed swab prevalence was 27.8%.
  2. State assignment. Swabbing was a random subsample of captures (588 of 1369), so the probability of recording a toad's status cannot depend on its state. The published estimates are nonetheless δ_U = 1.000 (at the boundary) and δ_I = 0.119.

Both checks fit one explanation: the fitted model treats captures of unknown status as infected toads. Without the data this cannot be tested on the real histories. palomar2023_swabbing_experiment tests it on simulated histories with the same design. Survival is set equal in both states, and the years without swabbing (2006, 2007, 2017 and 2018) are included. That experiment is our analysis, not a published result.

In one simulated data set (seed 2023), the published structure gave δ_U = 1.000, δ_I = 0.112, ψ_U→I = 0.66 and ψ_I→U = 0.25, close to the published values. The true values were δ = 0.43 in both states, ψ_U→I = 0.2 and ψ_I→U = 0.6. Survival was not biased. Declaring the unswabbed years unassessed recovered the transitions.

Hollanders et al. 2023: load-dependent mortality in continuous time

The states are alive Bd−, alive Bd+ and dead. All rates are hazards per six weeks. The infected mortality hazard is φ₂ = φ₁ exp(β_status + β_load x), where x is the frog's log10 load (ITS1 copies per swab) at the start of the interval. The load is centred at 3.174 and divided by 2 × 0.911. Recapture is modelled per secondary survey. Frogs were not swabbed on the last primary occasion, so an infected frog seen then is recorded as seen and Bd−.

The paper's printed text and the authors' code differ in several places, and the code is followed throughout. The differences are listed in src/models/hollanders2023.jl. Two matter for comparisons.

  • The printed slopes are posterior summaries conditional on RJMCMC inclusion.
  • The annual hazard is the six-weekly hazard × 52/6.

Chytridiomycosis.CTMultistateProblem — Type
CTMultistateProblem(data = load_hollanders2023(); nodes = (load = 5, individual = 3,
                    detection = 7), swab_final = false)

Reduced maximum-likelihood refit of the continuous-time robust-design multistate model of Hollanders et al. (2023). Each frog's capture history is an HMM over (alive Bd−, alive Bd+, dead) between primary occasions, with transition matrices ctmc_transition(infection_generator(ψ12, ψ21, φ1, φ2), τ) over the unequal intervals τ (six-week units) and recapture at each secondary survey. Conditioned on first capture. Kept as in the authors' code:

  • site-specific intercepts of mortality, gaining, clearing (log six-weekly hazard) and recapture (logit), covariates centred and divided by two sd as coded;
  • mortality: interval temperature, Bd status and load (log10 ITS1 copies per swab at the start of the interval, scaled); gaining: temperature and rainfall; clearing: temperature, rainfall and load; recapture: sex, survey-day temperature, rainfall, Bd status and load, and a normal individual effect;
  • frogs not swabbed on the final primary (swab_final = false: a Bd+ frog seen then is "seen, Bd−", the code's δ).

Missing loads (every occasion without a positive swab) are integrated out, not imputed by MCMC: the load submodel (normal, with temperature, rainfall and a normal individual effect, fitted first by maximum likelihood to the observed loads; hollanders2023_load_model) gives, for each frog, the distribution of its individual effect given its observed loads, and the latent loads are integrated by Gauss–Hermite quadrature (nodes.load per occasion, nodes.individual over the individual load effect, nodes.detection over the recapture individual effect). Unknown sex (15 frogs) is a two-point mixture at the observed female fraction. Dropped: site and survey random effects (sd 0.33, 0.49, 0.29 on the log hazards), the secondary-survey effects on recapture, body condition and every interaction, and RJMCMC. Parameters are maximised over the unconstrained vector CTMS_NAMES (26).

solve(prob) returns a CTMultistateFit. It is a LogDensityProblems target whose log density is the log-likelihood (flat prior).

source
Chytridiomycosis.hollanders2023_load_model — Function
hollanders2023_load_model(data = load_hollanders2023()) -> NamedTuple

Maximum-likelihood fit of the authors' imputation submodel to the 248 observed loads: the scaled log10 load x = (log10 L − mean)/(2 sd) of frog i at primary t is Normal(β_T temp_t + β_R rain_t + u_i, σ²) with u_i ~ Normal(0, σ_u²) and no intercept (the code fixes load.alpha = 0). The temperature × rainfall term, included in 55% of the authors' RJMCMC draws, is left out. Returns temperature, rain, sd (σ), sd_individual (σu) and loglik, on the scaled-load scale (authors' posterior medians: −0.359, 0.231, precision 5.85 i.e. σ = 0.41, and σu = 0.241).

Slopes (phi_temperature, phi_status, phi_load, gain_temperature, gain_rain, clear_temperature, clear_rain, clear_load, p_status, p_load) are on the log-hazard or logit scale per two sd of the covariate; mortality, gain, clearance are six-weekly hazards at mean covariates, geometric means over the sites; annual_hazard = mortality × 52/6, annual_survival, gain_probability, clearance_probability (1 − exp(−rate)); recapture (inverse logit of the mean site intercept); mortality_sites, gain_sites, clearance_sites (vectors of estimates); hazard_ratio at 10^5 and 10^6 ITS1 copies per swab (load_hazard_ratio); and the fitted load_model.

hollanders2023_fit(; refit = false) -> CTMultistateFit

The reduced maximum-likelihood refit (CTMultistateProblem with default options, started from hollanders2023_start). By default it is read from the stored result (hollanders2023_refit_path), so the validation targets do not re-optimise; the log-likelihood at the stored optimum is checked against the model in the tests. With refit = true, or if the file is missing, the fit is recomputed (several minutes; set JULIA_NUM_THREADS to use more than one thread). The result is cached for the session.

What was reproduced

  • From the bundled data. The sampling summary (686 frogs, 1609 primary detections, 248 of them infected, and the load range) is reproduced. So are the counts of observed gains and clearances.
  • From the authors' posterior draws. Every printed median and HDI that was checked is reproduced: the status and load effects, the annual hazard and survival, and the gain and clearance rates and probabilities. The hazard ratios printed in the Discussion are not: 4.4 at 10⁵ and 11 at 10⁶ ITS1 copies per swab, against about 6.4 and 19 from the draws.
  • From the reduced refit (maximum likelihood, 26 parameters, about 10 minutes on two threads; 593 s recorded in data/hollanders2023/refit.toml).
    • Matched within one posterior sd: the clearance hazard (2.62 against 2.623), the gain hazard (0.23 against 0.283), the intensity effect (1.29 against 1.953) and temperature on clearing (1.11 against 1.101).
    • Outside one posterior sd: the status effect (1.22 against −0.167), the annual hazard (0.67 against 0.783) and rainfall on gaining (1.93 against 1.141), although all three published values lie inside our Wald intervals.
    • The intensity effect, though within one sd, is not 'present' by the paper's rule: Wald P(β ≤ 0) = 0.12 against the paper's pd of 97.5%.
    • The two effects are confounded (sampling correlation −0.89). Fixing status at −0.167 lowers the log-likelihood by only 0.11, and the profiled fit gives intensity 2.58 and annual hazard 0.81.
